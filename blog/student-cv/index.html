How to Write a Student CV With No Experience

You have more experience than you think. Here is how to write school, campus and volunteering work as evidence an employer can use.

The Tech Hut · Sunday, 15 November 2026

A student CV with no work experience should lead with what you can prove: your education, projects, attachments, volunteering, clubs and skills. Keep it to one page, put a short profile at the top that says what you are looking for, and write every activity as if it were a job, with a title, dates and a result. Employers hiring students and graduates expect little experience; they look for evidence that you can learn, finish things and work with people.

This guide shows what to include, how to turn school and campus activities into strong bullets, and a short sample you can copy. For the general method, see our main guide on how to write a CV.

What to include on a student CV

SectionWhat goes in it
HeaderName, phone, a professional email, town, and a LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio link if you have one
ProfileTwo lines: your course and year, one thing you have done, what you want
EducationYour current course, institution and expected completion; KCSE grade if you are applying with no degree yet
ProjectsCoursework, final-year or personal projects, with a link or a result
ExperienceAttachments, part-time jobs, family business, volunteering
ActivitiesClubs, societies, sports leadership, competitions, hackathons
SkillsTools and skills the advert asks for that you can show

Leave out primary school, your date of birth, ID number and photo unless the advert asks for them.

Turning student activities into strong bullets

Use the same formula professionals use: action verb, what you did, result. Some examples:

  • Club role: “Treasurer, Computer Science Club: tracked a KSh 80,000 annual budget in Excel and published monthly statements to 60 members.”
  • Family business: “Set up WhatsApp Business and a price list for the family shop, bringing in about 15 repeat orders a week.”
  • Coursework: “Built a library management system in Java with two classmates; I designed the database and wrote the search feature.”
  • Volunteering: “Taught basic computer skills to 25 secondary students over a school holiday.”

You have more experience than you think. You have just not written it as experience yet.

A sample student CV

FAITH ACHIENG

Kisumu, Kenya | +254 7XX XXX XXX | faith.achieng@email.com | linkedin.com/in/faithachieng

PROFILE

Third-year IT student who set up a 20-computer lab for a local school. Looking for an IT support attachment from May 2027.

EDUCATION

BSc Information Technology | Maseno University | 2024 to 2028 (expected)

PROJECTS

School Computer Lab Set-up | Volunteer | Aug 2025

  • Installed and networked 20 donated computers and trained 4 teachers to use them

Student Helpdesk Tracker | Coursework | 2025

  • Built a ticket tracker in Google Sheets with Apps Script for a class of 40

ACTIVITIES

Secretary, IT Students' Association | 2025 to present

SKILLS

Windows and Linux basics, networking (LAN set-up), Google Workspace, Microsoft Office, customer service

Mistakes students make

  • Two pages of course units. List only modules that match the job.
  • “Hardworking, honest, team player.” Replace with one example that proves it.
  • A nickname email address. Use firstname.lastname.
  • One CV for every application. Change the profile and the order of projects for each.
  • A designed template with columns. Screening software often cannot read it. Use one column.
