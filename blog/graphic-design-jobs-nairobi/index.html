Graphic Design Jobs in Nairobi: Where to Find Work in 2026

In design hiring, your portfolio is the CV. Here is who hires designers in Nairobi, what they ask for, and how to get in front of them.

The Tech Hut · Sunday, 15 November 2026

Graphic design jobs in Nairobi are spread across four kinds of employer: agencies, in-house brand and marketing teams, NGOs and media houses, and tech companies hiring brand or product designers. Most roles ask for a portfolio before they ask for a degree, and many full-time roles now sit close to digital work such as social media, motion and UI. Freelance and contract work fills the gaps between them.

This guide covers who hires graphic designers in Nairobi, what the adverts ask for, where to look, and how to put together a portfolio that gets you interviews.

Who hires graphic designers in Nairobi

EmployerTypical workWhat they look for
Creative and advertising agenciesCampaigns, social content, print, pitches for many clientsSpeed, range, working to briefs and deadlines
In-house brand teamsMarketing assets for one company: banks, telcos, retailers, fintechsConsistency with brand guidelines, social and digital formats
NGOs and media housesReports, infographics, campaigns, editorial designClear information design, layout, sometimes video
Tech companiesBrand, marketing and product designDigital-first work, Figma, motion, sometimes UI

What is open right now

On 15 November 2026 our scan of employers' own hiring systems found design roles based in Kenya at One Acre Fund (a fixed-term graphic designer), Watu (a senior graphic designer and a brand project manager), Ajua (a product design lead) and Greenpeace International (a creative lead), among others. The mix shows where the market is: brand and graphic roles at fast-growing companies and NGOs, with product design increasingly listed alongside them.

What job adverts ask for

  • A portfolio link: almost every advert asks for one. It matters more than your certificate.
  • Tools: Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator and InDesign are standard; Figma is now common; After Effects or Premiere for motion; Canva for teams that produce at volume.
  • Formats: social media sizes, print, presentations and, increasingly, short video.
  • Experience: junior roles ask for one to two years or a strong portfolio; senior roles ask for brand ownership and managing other designers.

In design hiring, your portfolio is the CV. The CV is just the cover sheet.

A portfolio that gets interviews

  1. Show six to eight projects, not forty. Pick work that matches the jobs you want.
  2. Tell the story of each one: the brief, your role, the constraints, the result. A line on results, such as more sign-ups or a client renewal, sets you apart.
  3. Show process: sketches, options you rejected and why.
  4. Include real-world formats: a social campaign at the right sizes, a print piece, a brand system.
  5. Host it somewhere easy to open: Behance, Dribbble or a simple personal site, with a PDF version for email.

If you lack client work, design for a real local business or NGO with their permission, or redesign something you use daily and explain your thinking. Label self-initiated work honestly.

Where to find graphic design jobs in Nairobi

  • Our free jobs board: roles read directly from Kenyan and African employers' hiring systems; search “design”.
  • General boards: BrighterMonday, Fuzu and MyJobMag list agency and in-house roles.
  • LinkedIn: follow agencies and brands you would like to work for, and their creative leads.
  • Design communities: local designer communities and meetups share roles before they are advertised. Find some on our tech communities page and our events page.
  • Remote work: global companies hire designers remotely too; our Remote-From-Kenya list includes design roles open to people in Kenya.

Freelancing alongside a job search

Many Nairobi designers combine a job search with freelance clients. Agree the scope, number of revisions, deadline and price in writing before you start, ask for a deposit on larger jobs, and keep the source files until you are paid. Freelance work you are proud of goes straight into your portfolio.

Where to start

Trim your portfolio to your best six projects and write a short case study for each. Then set a weekly routine: one search on our jobs board, one on a general board, and one message to a creative lead whose work you admire. If you are just starting out, read our guide to graphic design internships in Nairobi.
