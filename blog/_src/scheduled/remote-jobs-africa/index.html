Remote Jobs in Africa: Where to Find Roles You Can Actually Get

Most remote jobs are remote only for people in the US or Europe. Here is where the roles open to Africa come from, and how to spot them in ten seconds.

The Tech Hut · {{DATE_LONG}}

The best remote jobs in Africa are the ones whose location line says “worldwide”, “EMEA” or names an African country, at employers who already pay people on the continent. On 15 November 2026 our daily scan of 3,730 live openings found 483 remote roles at 51 companies that would employ someone living in Kenya. That is about one in eight. The other seven in eight were remote only for people already living in the US, Europe or another named country.

This guide shows where Africa-friendly remote roles come from, which kinds of work dominate, how to read a location line in ten seconds, and how pay and contracts usually work when the employer is abroad.

What counts as a remote job open to Africa

A remote role is open to you only if the employer can legally pay you where you live. Employers do that in one of three ways: they have their own company in your country, they hire you as an independent contractor, or they use an employer-of-record service that employs you locally on their behalf. If the advert does not hint at any of these, it is usually closed to Africa, whatever the word “remote” suggests.

In our 15 November count, the 483 eligible roles split like this by location line:

Location line on the advertRolesWhat it means
Worldwide, anywhere or global315Open to any country the employer can pay
EMEA168Europe, the Middle East and Africa, usually with European working hours

“Remote” describes where the work happens. The location line tells you who is allowed to do it.

What kinds of remote work are open to Africans

Engineering dominates. Of the 483 roles, 254 were engineering and developer jobs, 42 were data and AI roles, 24 were in sales, 20 in product management and 18 in design. Finance, marketing, people and customer support made up most of the rest, in smaller numbers.

The employers are concentrated too. One open-source software company, Canonical, accounted for 239 of the 483 roles. AI talent company micro1 had 66 and Supabase had 49. That concentration is normal for remote-first hiring, and it is a reason to track employers rather than job boards: a handful of companies hire across Africa again and again.

Where to find remote roles open to Africa

1. Employers' own careers pages

The most reliable source is the employer's own hiring system, because that is where the location rules are written. Remote-first companies that hire in Africa list roles on systems such as Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby. Bookmark the careers pages of companies that have hired people you know, and check them weekly.

2. The Tech Hut Remote-From-Kenya list

Our Remote-From-Kenya list is that same check done for you every morning: each role, the apply link and the wording that shows the employer will take someone in Kenya. Most roles that are open to Kenya are open to other African countries too, but always read the location line, because some employers list specific countries. The list costs KSh 500 once at the launch price, with lifetime updates.

3. Remote job aggregators

Himalayas, Jobicy and Remote OK collect remote roles from many employers and let you filter by region. They are useful for volume. The catch is that region filters depend on how each employer tagged the role, so treat a filter as a shortlist, not a guarantee.

4. Talent networks

Networks such as Andela, Turing and micro1 vet developers and other specialists once, then match them to client projects. You apply to the network, pass its tests and wait for matches. It suits experienced engineers who want contract work without applying role by role.

5. African employers with remote teams

Fintechs and startups headquartered in Nairobi, Lagos, Cape Town or Cairo often hire remotely across the continent, especially for engineering and customer operations. Our free jobs board has a “Rest of Africa” filter that shows these alongside Kenyan roles.

How to read a location line in ten seconds

  • Open: “Worldwide”, “Anywhere”, “Remote, Africa”, “EMEA”, “Home based – Worldwide”, or a list of countries that includes yours.
  • Closed: “Remote, United States”, “Remote (UK)”, “Remote within Germany”, “Must have the right to work in the EU”.
  • Unclear: plain “Remote”. Search the description for “time zone”, “eligible” or “authorised to work”. If you still cannot tell, ask the recruiter before you spend an evening on the application.

Watch for a language requirement too. A worldwide role that needs fluent German or Portuguese is not open to most applicants.

Spend ten seconds on the location line before you spend an hour on the cover letter.

Pay, contracts and time zones

Most remote roles open to Africa are contracts, paid in US dollars or euros, either directly to a bank account or through an employer-of-record or payments platform. Before you accept, get in writing: the rate and currency, how often you are paid, which platform pays you and its fees, whether you are a contractor or an employee, and how much overlap with the team's hours is expected.

East Africa Time is UTC+3. EMEA roles usually overlap your working day comfortably. US-based teams often expect afternoon and evening hours in Kenya. Foreign income can carry Kenyan tax obligations, so confirm your position with a tax adviser before your first payment.

Where to start

Pick two sources: one that checks eligibility for you and one with volume. Save a search on each, read new roles once a week, and apply only where the location line says you can be hired.
