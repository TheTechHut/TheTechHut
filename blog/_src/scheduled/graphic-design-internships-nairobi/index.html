Graphic Design Internships in Nairobi: How to Land One

Many design internships are never advertised. A small, focused portfolio and a polite direct message get you further than mass applications.

The Tech Hut · {{DATE_LONG}}

Graphic design internships in Nairobi are mostly found at agencies, in-house marketing teams, NGOs and media houses. Few are advertised widely: many agencies take interns through referrals, portfolio reviews and direct messages, and larger employers open formal internship intakes only once or twice a year. A small, focused portfolio and a direct, polite approach get you further than mass applications.

This guide covers where design internships come from, what employers look for in an intern's portfolio, how to approach an agency directly, and what to ask before you accept.

Where design internships come from

SourceHow they hire interns
Creative agenciesOften informally: portfolio reviews, referrals, direct approaches
In-house marketing teamsThrough formal internship or attachment intakes on careers pages
NGOs and media housesCommunications internships, often with a fixed deadline
StartupsWhen a launch or campaign needs hands; usually on LinkedIn or their careers page

If you are still studying, an industrial attachment is the formal route: your institution provides a letter and the placement counts towards your course. If you have graduated, look for internships and junior designer roles.

What an intern's portfolio needs

Employers do not expect an intern to have client work. They look for taste, basic tool skills and evidence that you can follow a brief. Four to six projects are enough:

  • A social media set: three or four posts for one brand, in the correct sizes.
  • A small brand identity: a logo, colours, type and two applications, for a real or invented business.
  • A print or layout piece: a poster, menu, flyer or report page.
  • One piece of motion or UI, if you can: a short animated post or a simple app screen in Figma.

Write two or three lines under each: the brief, your choices and what you would change. Label school and self-initiated projects as such. Honesty here is noticed.

How to approach an agency directly

  1. Pick ten agencies or brands whose work you admire. Note one recent campaign of theirs.
  2. Find the creative director or design lead on LinkedIn or the agency's site.
  3. Send a short message: who you are, the campaign you liked and why, a link to your portfolio, and the dates you are available.
  4. Ask for a portfolio review, not just a job. Feedback is easier to say yes to.
  5. Follow up once after a week. Then move on politely.

Ask a design lead for feedback on your portfolio. Internships often start there.

What to ask before you accept

  • Is there a stipend, and how much? Many internships pay a stipend; some do not. Know before you commit.
  • How long is it, and what are the hours and office days?
  • Who will review your work and give feedback?
  • Will you work on live client or brand projects you can show afterwards?
  • Is there a chance of a junior role at the end?

Be careful with any “internship” that asks you to pay a fee to join, or that is unpaid full-time work with no learning or feedback.

Building skills while you search

If you want structured practice with mentors, The Tech Hut Apprenticeship has a UI/UX design track and a content creation track: 8 weeks, ending with a team project you can put in your portfolio. Design communities and meetups, listed on our events and communities pages, are also where many interns hear about openings first.

Where to start

Build a four-project portfolio this month, send five direct messages to design leads, and check our free jobs board and daily job alert for junior design roles. When you are ready for full-time work, read our guide to graphic design jobs in Nairobi.
