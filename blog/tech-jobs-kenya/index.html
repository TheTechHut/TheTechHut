Tech Jobs in Kenya: 5 Best Places to Find Them in 2026

The jobs are there, but scattered across dozens of sites. Five places to look, who each one suits, what it costs, and where to start if you have no experience.

The Tech Hut · Sunday, 15 November 2026

The best places to find tech jobs in Kenya show live openings, link straight to the employer's application, and let you filter by city and experience level. Start with The Tech Hut jobs board for a free, fresh list of Kenyan roles. Add LinkedIn if you have two or more years of experience. Use Fuzu and the other general boards for IT jobs in Nairobi and entry-level support work. Watch public-sector adverts if you are a recent graduate. Check tech-only and startup boards for developer roles and internships.

The hard part is not a lack of jobs. Listings are scattered across dozens of sites, many are expired reposts, and "entry level" adverts often ask for three years of experience. Remote roles frequently exclude Kenyan applicants, so you can spend an evening on an application that was never open to you.

This article walks through five sources, one per section. For each you get what it lists, who it suits, and what it costs, with notes for readers who have little or no experience. The Tech Hut is our own project, so it comes first, and we also say where it falls short.

1. The Tech Hut jobs board

The Tech Hut jobs board is a free list of live tech openings read directly from employers' own hiring systems, so you apply on the employer's page and not through a middleman.

What you find there

The board reads systems such as Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Pinpoint, SmartRecruiters, Workday and SuccessFactors. It is rebuilt every three hours, and you can filter by role, type and date, and switch between Kenya, the rest of Africa and remote.

Employers it tracks include Safaricom, KCB, Equity, Absa and Old Mutual. The limit is that employers whose recruitment systems cannot be read automatically are not listed. The board links to their own portals instead, so pair it with one more source from this list.

A board that rebuilds through the day keeps you from applying to roles that closed last week.

Who it suits

It suits anyone in Nairobi or elsewhere in Kenya who wants one place for roles they can realistically take. The free daily job alert emails new openings pulled from employers' career pages every morning, which helps if you check jobs on your phone between other things.

If you have no experience at all, the Apprenticeship is the entry route. It is an 8-week programme with four tracks: software development, digital marketing, UI/UX design and content creation. The first four weeks build foundations and the last four put you on a team project with mentors. You finish with certifications and portfolio-ready work. Applying is an online form.

2. LinkedIn Jobs

LinkedIn is the best source for mid-level and senior tech roles at multinationals, banks and fintechs, because recruiters search it as well as post on it.

What you find there

Its tech jobs page for Kenya shows more than 1,000 open roles. Sample titles include Platform Engineer, Senior Backend Engineer and Azure Administrator and Technical Support Engineer. The mix leans toward engineering and experienced hires, with Nairobi as the main location.

Many listings use Easy Apply, which sends your profile in a few clicks. That speed is also the catch. Hundreds of people can apply to the same role within days, so a tailored CV sent to the hiring manager often beats a fast click.

Who it suits

It suits developers, cloud and DevOps people, data engineers and product managers with two or more years of experience. It also suits anyone who wants recruiters to find them, since a complete profile with your stack and projects gets you inbound messages.

If you are starting out, set the experience level filter to internship or entry level and search for titles such as "graduate", "intern" or "junior". Expect fewer results than on general boards, and do not let that discourage you.

Cost and how to use it

A basic account is free and enough for job searching. Set it up in this order:

  1. Set the location to Nairobi or Kenya.
  2. Save your search and turn on job alerts for the titles you want.
  3. Add your stack, tools and two or three projects to your headline and about section.
  4. Message the recruiter or hiring manager after you apply.

On LinkedIn, the profile you build counts as much as the jobs you apply to.

3. General job boards: Fuzu, BrighterMonday and MyJobMag

General boards are the best place to look for IT jobs in Nairobi at the support, ICT officer and systems level, because they list far more than developer roles.

What you find there

Fuzu has a dedicated information technology, software development and data category. It filters by city (Nairobi, Nakuru, Kisumu, Kilifi and others), by seniority (entry and basic, mid, senior) and by workplace (hybrid, on-site, remote). It also shows how many days are left to apply. Recent roles included IT Specialist, IT Support Specialist, Senior Support Engineer and a data analytics intern post.

BrighterMonday has an engineering and technology category with 158 vacancies in a recent look. MyJobMag has an ICT and computer category covering software engineer, systems analyst, business analyst, network engineer, technical support and web developer roles.

Who it suits

These boards suit people looking for entry-level tech jobs in Kenya, especially in IT support, networks and systems administration. Search on titles that employers use for starter roles:

  • IT support
  • ICT officer
  • Help desk
  • Intern or graduate trainee
  • Junior system administrator
  • Data entry to data analyst (internal moves)

Be realistic about "no experience" claims. Many adverts that say entry level still want a diploma or degree and some hands-on exposure, so a school project, a volunteer IT job or a certification belongs at the top of your CV.

Cost and how to use it

Browsing is free on all three. Fuzu asks you to join before it shows full job details, so create a profile once and turn on alerts.

Check the posted date and the apply-by date before you spend time on an application. Boards that repost adverts can show roles that have already closed.

For IT jobs in Nairobi, the general boards hold the support and ICT officer roles that developer-only sites miss.

4. Government and public-sector ICT roles

Government and public-sector adverts are one of the most realistic routes for graduates with little experience, because many ICT officer posts are graded by qualification and not by years in industry.

What you find there

State agencies, regulators and county governments regularly advertise ICT roles. Recent examples include ICT Officer I and II posts in software development and cybersecurity at the Digital Health Agency, an ICT officer post at the Insurance Regulatory Authority, and ICT support roles at county governments such as Laikipia and Makueni. Most are full-time and Nairobi-based, with some in counties.

The ICT Authority also runs the Presidential Digital Talent Programme, which places fresh ICT and engineering graduates inside government ministries for hands-on experience.

Who it suits

It suits recent graduates with a degree in computer science, IT, ICT or telecommunications engineering. Some posts, such as ICT Officer II, list a bachelor's degree and no stated years of experience. Senior posts ask for 6 to 15 years, so read the grade before you apply.

You also need patience. Public adverts have fixed deadlines, set application instructions and sometimes an advert number you must quote. Missing one detail can disqualify you.

Cost and how to use it

Applying is free. Watch the careers pages of the ICT Authority and the agencies you want to work for, along with the adverts that boards repost.

Follow the advert exactly: the subject line, the documents, the certificates and the deadline. Then prepare for a panel interview that tests fundamentals such as networking, systems and security basics.

Public-sector ICT adverts reward people who follow the instructions to the letter.

5. Tech-only boards and startup listings

Tech-only boards and startup listings are the best place to find developer, data and product roles, plus internships at global tech firms, without the noise of general boards.

What you find there

Coding Kenya is a Kenya-focused tech board with categories such as backend engineer, artificial intelligence, automation, big data and blockchain developer. It has also listed internships, including an AWS Kenya cloud support associate internship, plus roles from Postbank and the International Rescue Committee.

Wellfound lists tech and startup jobs in Kenya from companies such as KOKO Networks and VertoFX. It is more useful for startup roles and equity-style offers than for corporate ICT posts.

Who it suits

Both suit developers, data people and product managers who prefer startup or product-company work. Coding Kenya's internship listings are a realistic way in if you have no experience but can show a portfolio, a GitHub account or a finished project.

If you want startup-style work but have no portfolio yet, build one first. Two real projects beat a long list of tutorials, and they give you something concrete to send.

Cost and how to use it

Both are free for job seekers. Create a profile on each, save a search for your stack, and apply directly through the employer link when one is given.

Tailor each application to the role. Startups read fewer applications than banks, so a short note that names a product problem you could help with stands out.

At a startup, a specific note about the product often carries more weight than a long CV.

Where to start this week

The right search is a short stack, not a single site. Use one source that shows fresh Kenyan roles, one that fits your level, and one niche board for your skill. If you have no experience, aim at support, ICT officer, internship and apprenticeship roles, and put proof of work at the top of your CV. If you have experience, LinkedIn and the tech-only boards will serve you better.

Whichever you pick, check the posted date, confirm Kenyan applicants are eligible, and format your CV so hiring software can read it. Applying fast to a few right roles beats applying slowly to many wrong ones.
