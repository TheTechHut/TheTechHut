Landing tech jobs in Kenya: the real talk

From startups to established companies, openings in Kenyan tech are sprouting faster than maize after the long rains. Getting one of them is another matter. This guide covers what is in your favour, the hard truths, and a plan you can follow to land the job.

The good news: now is a good time to start

In your favour · 01

AI is here, so use it

Use AI tools to learn, to get help with code, to write up the projects in your portfolio and to tighten your job applications. Used well, they let you learn faster and get more done. Use them; don't fear them.

In your favour · 02

You're young, and you have execution energy

Youth counts for a lot in tech. You have the raw energy, adaptability, and often fewer entrenched habits, making you agile learners and quick implementers. This "execution energy" is highly valued, so channel it into building, learning and networking.

The harsh realities

"The known bird gets the job"

This is perhaps the biggest reality in Kenya. Networking is not just important; it's often everything. Many jobs are filled through referrals or within existing networks before they even hit public job boards.

Solution: be the known bird.

This isn't about unfairness; it's about strategy. Your goal is to become known within the tech community.

Your first job? You'll likely be underpaid

It's a common story. Companies often leverage the high competition and your lack of "local" experience.

Strategy:

Go in for the experience. Draw clear boundaries on your time and energy to avoid burnout. View it as an accelerated learning bootcamp. Once you have 6-12 months of solid experience, your market value will jump significantly.

Jobs are highly competitive, so monetise side projects

With many talented people going for a limited number of positions, you have to stand out.

Strategy:

Don't just build side projects; try to monetise them, even if it's a small amount. This shows entrepreneurial spirit, problem-solving, market understanding and initiative, which are the qualities employers look for. It also gives you a fallback if the job hunt takes longer than expected.

The blueprint: seven steps to a tech job

Build skills based on JDs

Stop learning randomly. Find 5-10 job descriptions for roles you aspire to, highlight recurring skills, and build your learning path around them.

Build online presence: personal brand

Optimise LinkedIn, showcase projects on GitHub or a portfolio, and consider a personal website or blog to house your work and thoughts.

Build offline presence: reputation

Attend meetups, workshops, and conferences. Volunteer, contribute to discussions, and genuinely connect with people to build your reputation.

Be active on tech WhatsApp groups

These are goldmines in Kenya. Engage in discussions and apply for jobs immediately when they are posted. Speed is crucial.

Upskill and share your story publicly

Continuously learn new technologies. Share your journey on LinkedIn, Twitter, a blog, or WhatsApp groups to build your brand and show dedication.

In interviews, build relationships

Communicate value ("Here's how I can solve your problems"). Negotiate on value, not your situation. Research salary benchmarks.

Lead with confidence

Believe in your abilities. Even if you're new, project an aura of readiness and competence. Your mindset is contagious; if you believe you belong, others will too.

Where to start

Landing a tech job in Kenya is a marathon, not a sprint. It takes deliberate effort, continuous learning and a willingness to put yourself out there. Use what is in your favour, prepare for the hard parts and work through the blueprint. The jobs are there, and with the right approach you can build a good career in Kenya's tech industry.
