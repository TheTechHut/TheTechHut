IT Internships in Nairobi: Where to Find Them and How to Apply

Internships come in waves. Know which employers open them, when, and what they screen for, and arrive with proof you can do the work.

The Tech Hut · Tuesday, 17 November 2026

IT internships in Nairobi come in waves, not as a steady stream. Banks, telcos and state agencies open their internship and attachment programmes once or twice a year, startups hire interns when a project needs hands, and global tech companies with Nairobi teams post a few graduate roles at a time. The way to land one is to know which of those three you are targeting, watch them on a schedule, and arrive with proof that you can do the work.

This guide covers where IT internships in Nairobi are advertised, the difference between an internship and an attachment, what employers screen for, and what to do while you wait for the next intake.

Internship, attachment or graduate trainee?

TypeWho it is forTypical length
Industrial attachmentStudents still enrolled, as a required part of a diploma or degreeAbout 8 to 12 weeks
InternshipRecent graduates, sometimes final-year studentsUsually 3 to 12 months
Graduate traineeGraduates hired into a structured programme that can lead to a permanent roleOften 12 months or more

Read the advert for the word the employer uses. An attachment advert usually asks for a letter from your institution; an internship advert asks for your degree or diploma certificate.

Where IT internships in Nairobi are advertised

Large employers' careers pages

Safaricom, the major banks and other large employers run internship programmes and publish them on their own careers pages, usually with a fixed deadline. Government bodies do the same: the Kenya Revenue Authority, for example, advertises industrial attachment intakes by period. Make a list of ten employers you would like to start at and check their careers pages every two weeks.

Public-sector programmes

The ICT Authority's Presidential Digital Talent Programme places ICT and engineering graduates in government ministries for hands-on experience. County governments and state agencies also advertise ICT internships. Public adverts are strict about documents and deadlines, so follow every instruction exactly.

Startups and tech companies

Startups rarely run formal programmes. They hire interns when they need them and often post the role on their own careers page or LinkedIn only. On 15 November 2026 our scan of employers' hiring systems found a customer support internship at Sun King among the Kenya roles. Global companies with Nairobi teams post graduate and early-career roles too, though most internships at global firms are tied to their head-office country.

General job boards

Fuzu, BrighterMonday and MyJobMag repost many internship adverts. Check the posted date and the closing date before you apply, since reposts can outlive the original deadline.

What employers screen for

Internship adverts ask for little experience, but employers still shortlist on evidence. The applicants who get calls usually show three things:

  • One relevant project. A small app, a network you set up, a dashboard, a cleaned dataset. Link it from your CV.
  • The basics of the stack in the advert. If it names SQL, Linux, Python or Active Directory, say where you have used it, even in coursework.
  • A clean, readable CV. One or two pages, one column, no graphics. Our guide on how to write a CV covers the format step by step.

An intern who has built one thing beats an intern who lists ten courses.

How to apply so you stand out

  1. Save the advert as a PDF the day you see it. Adverts get taken down before deadlines.
  2. Match your CV to it: use the same names for the tools and skills it asks for, where they are true for you.
  3. Send exactly the documents requested, named clearly, before the deadline.
  4. Write a three-sentence cover note: the role, one reason you fit, and your availability dates.
  5. Keep a simple tracker of where you applied and when, and follow up once after two weeks if there is a contact.

What to do between intakes

Most of the year there is no internship open at the employer you want. Use that time to build the evidence that wins the next one. Enter a hackathon from our hackathons list, join a tech community and its meetups, or apply for The Tech Hut Apprenticeship, an 8-week programme with mentors and a team project in software development, digital marketing, UI/UX design or content creation.
