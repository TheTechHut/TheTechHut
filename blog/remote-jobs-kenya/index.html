8 Best Places to Find Legit Remote Jobs in Kenya in 2026

Most “remote” listings are not open to you. Here is where to look for the ones that are, who each source suits, and how to tell a real job from a scam.

The Tech Hut · Saturday, 14 November 2026

The best sources of remote jobs in Kenya are the ones that state, in writing, that they hire people who live in Kenya, and that link to the employer's real application page. Our top pick, The Tech Hut's Remote-From-Kenya list, suits Kenyans who are done wasting applications. Jobgether and Remote Rocketship suit wide searches across many fields. Arc suits developers, BrighterMonday suits beginners who want local employers, and Opentrain suits people with a technical skill who want flexible task work.

The problem is that most remote listings are not open to you. "Remote" often means remote within the US, the UK or the EU, and applying to those costs you weeks and brings no reply. Scams that promise easy online money crowd the same search results, and they target people with no experience hardest.

This article covers eight places to find remote jobs in Kenya in 2026. For each one you get what it offers, who it suits and what to watch for. Then it matches the sources to your situation, whether you have no experience, want part-time or task work, or are aiming at UK and US employers. It ends with a check for telling a legit job from a scam. We put our own list first because it is built for this exact problem, and we say where it falls short.

1. The Tech Hut Remote-From-Kenya list

What it offers

Each role comes with the direct apply link and the wording that proves the employer accepts Kenyan applicants. It comes from a scan of more than 3,700 live openings, repeated daily, and it is updated every morning.

  • The list itself: KSh 500 as a one-time payment at the launch price (regular price KSh 1,000), with lifetime updates. You pay by M-Pesa or card.
  • Free alongside it: the jobs board, which you can filter by Kenya, the rest of Africa or remote. There is also a free daily job alert and a WhatsApp channel with over 9,000 followers.
  • Optional: Early Access from KSh 250 a month shows you every opening 24 hours before the public channel, screened for scams and expiry.
  • Bundle: the Job Hunt Bundle costs KSh 1,500 and covers the list, the CV Blueprint, one ATS Pass, the African Tech Community Database and three months of Early Access. Bought separately, those cost KSh 2,600.

A remote job only counts if the employer says it will hire you in Kenya.

Who it suits and what to watch

This list suits Kenyans in an active search who want to apply only where they can be hired. That includes developers, data people, product managers and operations staff. It also fits anyone who has already been burned by a "remote" role that quietly excluded Kenya.

The catch is volume. A curated list is smaller than an aggregator with thousands of postings, and because The Tech Hut is a tech career hub, coverage is strongest in tech roles. The free board's remote filter also shows remote roles in general, so read each location line before you apply. The paid list is the one built around proof of Kenya eligibility.

2. Jobgether

What it offers

Jobgether's Kenya page tracked 11,868 remote roles, the largest pool of any source here. You can browse by category and filter by contract type (full time, fixed term, freelance) and seniority. The salary range shown across these roles runs from $19,000 to $120,000 a year, which tells you how wide the spread is.

  • Biggest categories: banking, investment and insurance (603), AI and machine learning (272), software engineer (199), legal, risk and compliance (174), account executive (167).
  • Location tags: each listing says where the employer hires from, such as "Remote from Africa".
  • Access: a free account unlocks the full list, and a paid tier adds auto-apply.

Jobgether gives you the biggest pool, so use its tags to shrink it to roles open to Kenya.

Who it suits and what to watch

Jobgether suits mid-level and senior professionals in finance, legal, sales, engineering and AI who want volume. The contract filter also makes it useful for anyone hunting freelance or fixed-term work.

Be careful with the headline count. The Kenya page also surfaces roles tagged "Remote from Europe" and "Remote from South America", so the Kenya filter is not a guarantee. Many listings also come through staffing and outsourcing firms. Read the tag and the description before you spend an hour tailoring an application.

3. Remote Rocketship

What it offers

Remote Rocketship lists 1,785 positions for Kenya, and its location tags are the useful part. "Kenya - Remote" means the employer hires there, while "Anywhere in the World" means open worldwide. Each listing also carries a ghost score, an estimate of the chance that nobody is really hiring, where 60% or higher flags a probable ghost job.

  • Salary data: a breakdown by experience level, with figures for junior, mid-level, senior and lead roles.
  • Hiring speed: employers took about 46 days to close a role, based on 2,595 listings.
  • Skills in demand: across 4,133 postings, the most common were data analysis (10.9%), Python (9.5%), CI/CD (9.2%), AWS (9.0%) and customer service (8.3%).
  • Access: only about 20 listings show before you sign up. The remaining 1,766 sit behind registration.

Check the ghost score and the language tag before you write a single cover letter.

Who it suits and what to watch

Remote Rocketship suits tech, data and operations professionals, and anyone who wants openings that have not been flooded with applicants. The site says about half of its newest jobs are not on LinkedIn.

Several "Anywhere in the World" listings carry a language tag such as German or Portuguese Required, so open-to-all does not mean open-to-you. With a 46-day typical hiring time, apply to several roles at once instead of waiting on one. Always click through to the employer's own page to apply.

4. LinkedIn

What it offers

LinkedIn's remote page for Kenya showed 436 open roles, including senior backend engineer, platform engineer and AI engineer positions. It is the best place to see which employers are hiring and who posted the job. Set the location to Kenya, switch the workplace filter to remote, and save the search as a job alert.

  • Alerts: a daily email for each saved search.
  • People: the poster or recruiter is often visible, so you can message them directly.
  • Profile: recruiters search it, so a complete profile brings inbound offers.

On LinkedIn, the application is only half the work; message the person who posted the job.

Who it suits and what to watch

LinkedIn suits experienced professionals with a complete profile and a record of results they can point to. It is weaker for beginners, because popular postings draw large numbers of applicants.

The location filter can mislead you. A role may show Kenya yet be open only to residents of the employer's country. Search the description for "Kenya", "Africa" or "EMEA", and treat unsolicited recruiter messages with suspicion until you have verified the company on its own website.

5. BrighterMonday

What it offers

BrighterMonday is a Kenyan job board with a dedicated remote category, which listed 13 current vacancies. That is a small number, but the employers are local. You avoid the eligibility problem, the time-zone gap and the cross-border payment headaches that come with foreign employers.

  • Pay scale: expect local employers and local pay scales, usually in shillings.
  • Alerts: set one for the remote category so new vacancies reach you quickly.
  • Mix of roles: read each ad closely, because the board covers many fields rather than tech alone.

A short list of local remote roles beats a long list of roles that exclude you.

Who it suits and what to watch

BrighterMonday suits beginners and part-time seekers who want a Kenyan contract and an employer they can verify. It is also a sensible fallback while you wait on international applications.

Low volume is the main limit, so check it often. Pay will generally sit below what international employers offer. Some "remote" ads also turn out to be hybrid, so look for office-day requirements in the description before you commit.

6. Crossover

What it offers

Crossover has a Nairobi page for full-time remote work. The roles it showed included Executive Recruiter, VP of Program Management Office, AI-Driven Learning Analyst and AI Professional Services positions. The mix leans toward senior and specialist roles, and the listings are full-time.

  • Contract: full-time only, so it does not cover part-time searches.
  • Seniority: the titles assume a proven track record.
  • Details: pay, hours and role scope appear in each listing.

Crossover is for people with a track record, not for first-job seekers.

Who it suits and what to watch

Crossover suits experienced people in recruiting, program management, analytics and AI who want a full-time remote role and can show results.

Before you invest time, ask three things. Would you be a contractor or an employee? What hours and time-zone overlap does the role expect? How many hiring steps come before an offer? Get the answers in writing, then compare the pay with your market so you know what the offer is worth.

7. Arc

What it offers

Arc's Kenya page lists remote jobs at global tech companies, from freelance engagements to full-time roles. It is a tech-focused board, so you will find developers, designers and data roles instead of general admin work. The mix of freelance and full-time lets you choose between project income and a steady contract.

  • Employers: global tech companies, not local Kenyan firms.
  • Contract types: freelance, contract and full-time.
  • Fit: roles are built around technical skills, so listings assume you can show work.

Arc rewards proof of work, so lead with projects instead of a long CV.

Who it suits and what to watch

Arc suits developers, designers and data professionals with a portfolio, a GitHub profile or shipped projects. Freelancers who want to build a client base from Nairobi will find it useful.

Competition is global, so your application competes with candidates from many countries. It is no help if you work outside tech, and it is a poor fit for first-time job seekers. Confirm the time-zone overlap and the payment method before you accept, since many employers sit far from East Africa.

8. Opentrain

What it offers

Opentrain lists remote AI training and data labeling jobs open to people in Kenya. The sample projects on its Kenya page include Godot game development, gameplay systems and technical reviews, advertised at $70 to $130 per hour with flexible weekly hours. This is the closest thing here to remote task work, and it is paid by the hour.

  • Work type: project-based tasks, such as reviewing, labeling or building for AI training.
  • Hours: flexible by the week, which suits part-time schedules.
  • Pay: hourly, with rates shown on the listing.

Task work pays well only when you bring a skill that the project actually needs.

Who it suits and what to watch

Opentrain suits people with a technical or domain skill, such as coding, game development or specialist review, who want flexible side income. Those listed rates apply to specialist work. Basic data labeling pays far less, so check the rate and the expected hours in writing before you start.

Project work can end without warning, so never rely on one project as your only income. Also, never pay a fee to unlock tasks or training. Genuine platforms pay you.

How to match a source to your situation

Match the source of your remote jobs in Kenya to your experience level, your hours and your target employer's time zone. Beginners should start with local boards and entry-level roles, part-timers with flexible task platforms, and anyone targeting the UK or US with a time-zone check.

Remote jobs in Kenya with no experience

Entry-level remote work clusters in a few role types: customer support, virtual assistant work, general administration and translation. Jobgether's Kenya page gives a rough sense of volume, though these counts include all seniority levels and shift daily.

Applicants with no experience should lean on skills you can prove: typing speed, written English, comfort with Google Workspace, Zoom and Slack. Beginners also lose applications to resume-scanning software, so The Tech Hut's free CV Pack, which gives six formatting rules for getting past employers' applicant tracking systems, is worth reading before you apply.

With no experience, a clean CV and one proven skill beat fifty rushed applications.

Part-time, online and task work

For part-time and online work, use filters instead of guesswork. Jobgether's contract filter separates freelance and fixed-term roles from full-time ones, Arc lists freelance engagements, and Opentrain advertises flexible weekly hours. BrighterMonday suits local part-time roles, where there is no cross-border payment to arrange.

Treat any job whose entire pitch is easy tasks for quick money as a warning sign. Remote task jobs that pay well ask for a skill, a test or an interview. The ones that ask for a deposit first are the scams.

UK and US employers

Employers in the UK and US can hire you as a contractor, but the clock decides whether the job is livable. Kenya runs on East Africa Time, UTC+3, with no daylight saving. This table shows what a 9am to 5pm shift abroad means in Kenya during the current winter months.

Employer base Their 9am to 5pm Your time in Kenya
UK (GMT) 09:00 to 17:00 12:00 to 20:00
US East (EST) 09:00 to 17:00 17:00 to 01:00
US West (PST) 09:00 to 17:00 20:00 to 04:00

UK roles overlap your day comfortably. US roles often mean evening and night shifts, so ask for the required overlap hours before you apply. Daylight saving moves these figures by an hour in summer.

Whatever source you choose, set up a weekly routine so the search does not eat your life. Pick two sources from this list that fit your level, and save one search on each. Spend one session a week reading new listings, and apply only to roles where the location line names Kenya, Africa or a worldwide option with no language requirement. Keep a simple spreadsheet with the employer, the date you applied, the link to the original listing and the reply. After 10 days without a reply, send one short follow-up and move on.

Tailor the top third of your CV for each role. Recruiters scan the first lines, so put the skill the job asks for there, with a result you can prove. A cover note of three or four sentences that names the role and the one reason you fit beats a long letter. Keep your portfolio, GitHub or LinkedIn link in the header so nobody has to ask for it.

Finally, test your setup before an interview. Check your internet connection, keep mobile data as a backup, and join the video call a few minutes early. Employers hiring across borders judge reliability early, and a smooth call shows you can work alone.

How to tell a legit remote job from a scam

Legit remote jobs in Kenya name a real company, appear on that company's own careers page, and never ask you to pay to start. If any of those three fails, stop. These checks take ten minutes and save weeks.

Confirm the employer and the Kenya eligibility

Find the same listing on the employer's own website, then check the company's LinkedIn page for real employees and a history. Read the location line for Kenya, Africa or "anywhere" and confirm eligibility with the recruiter if it is vague. Insist on a video or phone interview. A hire that skips the interview is a red flag.

If you cannot find the job on the employer's own site, do not apply.

Red flags to walk away from

  • Fees: any charge for training, equipment, registration, a "starter kit" or unlocking tasks.
  • Money handling: requests to receive payments or cheques and forward them on.
  • Chat-only recruiting: an offer delivered only through WhatsApp or Telegram, with no company website or named recruiter.
  • Pay mismatch: very high pay for something like liking videos or filling surveys.
  • Pressure: "limited slots" or "reply within the hour".

Pay, contract and tax before you sign

Get the contract in writing. It should state your pay rate and currency, how and when you are paid, the expected hours, and whether you are a contractor or an employee. Ask what payment method the employer uses and what fees apply on your side. Foreign income can carry Kenyan tax obligations, so confirm your position with a local tax advisor before your first payment arrives.

Start with the sources that name Kenya

The pattern across all eight sources is the same. Remote jobs in Kenya are real, but they are buried under listings that exclude you, so favor sources that state where an employer hires and link to the real application. Use big aggregators for reach, local boards for easy entry, specialist platforms for task and tech work, and a ten-minute verification before every application.
