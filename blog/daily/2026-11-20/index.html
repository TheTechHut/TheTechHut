Kenya wants multinational investment, but its new 15% minimum tax changes the sums

The Daily Savannah · Friday, 20 November 2026 · 6 stories

Kenya's new 15% minimum tax on large multinationals clashes with the cheaper rates it offers to attract them, and the notification deadline is 30 November; KRA audits now reach five years back and into digital records; an Absa executive says payments alone will not pay banks' bills by 2030; Bending Spoons buys Mailchimp for US$3.68 billion; Google lets Gemini use your Mac; and a transcription firm used by police left recordings in an open cloud folder.

Kenya wants multinational investment, but its new 15% minimum tax changes the sums

To win regional headquarters, the Nairobi International Financial Centre offers qualifying holding companies a 15% corporate tax rate for their first ten years, rising to 20% for the next ten. At the same time, Kenya's Minimum Top-Up Tax, introduced in 2024 as part of a global agreement, makes sure large multinational groups pay an effective rate of at least 15% here. It applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR750 million, which RSM East Africa's Samuel Maina estimates at KSh100 to 110 billion, in at least two of the previous four years. The KRA published the rules in September, and the deadline to notify it has been extended from 17 to 30 November. Payments are due four months after the financial year ends. Maina says companies in export processing zones with ten-year tax holidays are particularly exposed, because the top-up can claw back the benefit of the holiday. Why it matters here: the multinationals affected are among Kenya's biggest employers of finance, tax and technology staff, and the rules decide how attractive Nairobi is for their next regional office. If you work in tax or finance at one of them, this month's deadline is yours.

KRA audits now reach five years back and draw on far more digital records

A full audit can cover up to five years, and initial requests often ask for answers within seven days, an administrative habit rather than a legal deadline, says Jilna Shah of RSM Eastern Africa. eTIMS invoices and other digital records give the taxman more to compare. If you invoice as a freelancer or run a small business, keep your records reconciled now. Objections go through iTax, and KRA has 60 days to decide.

BANKING

“Payments won't pay the bills by 2030,” says Absa's former M-PESA boss

Sitoyo Lopokoiyit, now head of personal and private banking at Absa Group, told the Africa Tech Festival that about 60% of transactions outside South Africa are already free, so banks must earn from lending, insurance and buy-now-pay-later built on payment rails. “Payments are the rails you have to play on,” he said. Absa Next, its digital platform open to non-customers, passed 600,000 sign-ups in Kenya about two months after launch.

DEALS

Bending Spoons buys Mailchimp from Intuit for US$3.68 billion

Intuit paid about US$12 billion for the email marketing company in 2021, so it is selling at less than a third of that. The Italian buyer, which has also bought AOL, Eventbrite, Airtable and Miro, says it takes “beloved brands” and makes them better; it is also known for deep job cuts after purchases. If your business runs its newsletter on Mailchimp, watch for price and plan changes.

TechCrunch

AI

Google lets Gemini use your Mac for you

The Gemini app for macOS can now open native apps and browse in the background to complete tasks, with a live preview you can pause or stop and approvals for actions. It is US-only and English-only for now, and needs a paid Google AI plan. OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta already offer similar desktop agents.

SECURITY

A transcription firm used by police and tech giants left recordings in an open cloud folder

San Francisco-based Tigerfish stored police interview transcripts, body-camera footage, customers' raw audio and invoices in an Amazon S3 bucket anyone could list without a password. A researcher reported it in October and got no reply; the company locked it after TechCrunch called. If you build on cloud storage, check today that your buckets are private.
