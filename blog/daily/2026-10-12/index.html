Microsoft's Africa Development Centre in Nairobi is reportedly closing

The Daily Savannah · Monday, 12 October 2026 · 5 stories

Staff at Microsoft's Nairobi engineering centre have reportedly been told it is closing — the company's own statement avoids the word — while the Dangote GDR offer shuts tomorrow at a KES 535 minimum and KRA beats its customs target.

Featured · Jobs

Microsoft's Africa Development Centre in Nairobi is reportedly closing

Employees who spoke to CIO Africa say they were told on Monday that the centre is closing. Microsoft's spokesperson described "proposed changes" within the company that "have impacted the engineering department in Kenya", and said Microsoft will keep operating in the country — a statement that neither uses the word "closure" nor denies it. Headcount, end date, affected teams and severance terms have not been disclosed. The centre opened in Nairobi and Lagos in May 2019 on a US$100 million, five-year pledge; its last published headcount, from March 2022, was more than 450 full-time staff, working on Identity, Microsoft 365, Windows and devices, security and developer tools from Dunhill Towers in Westlands. Lagos closed in 2024, when Microsoft said there were "no impacts" to Kenya. The same day, ICT Cabinet Secretary William Kabogo met a Microsoft delegation led by Jeff Bullwinkel and including East Africa lead Angela Ng'ang'a; he said he stressed protecting local talent and engineering jobs, Microsoft reaffirmed its long-term commitment, and the two sides will draft a joint action plan on AI in government, digital public services, cybersecurity, infrastructure and skills. It is not clear whether the meeting was prompted by the closure news. Microsoft cut about 4,800 roles globally in July with a warning that more changes were coming. An unverified LinkedIn account from 23 September describes staff being offered a performance improvement plan or a "voluntary" exit. For anyone affected, Kenyan law as Tech-ish describes it requires written notice to each employee and the labour officer, one month's notice or pay in lieu, leave pay and at least 15 days' pay per full year worked in severance.

Dangote refinery GDRs: the minimum drops from KES 107,000 to KES 535 — offer closes tomorrow

Renaissance Capital cut the minimum application from 2,000 GDRs to 10 at KES 53.50 each, citing investor feedback, strong demand and Capital Markets Authority approval; it matches what Nigerian investors paid in the refinery's Nigerian IPO. Each GDR represents one share in Dangote Petroleum Refinery Plc, held in custody in Nigeria, and trades in shillings on the NSE once listed. The offer covers up to 728,971,962 GDRs, roughly KES 39 billion, opened on 6 October and closes at 5 PM on 13 October. You need a CDSC account and can apply through *483*250#, the offer portal or an authorised agent, paying in full; applying does not guarantee receiving all the GDRs. The NSE listing still needs a go-ahead from Nigeria's SEC, with no trading date given. A lower minimum widens access but does not lower the price or remove the risks, and a crowd of applicants does not guarantee an active market afterwards.

GOVERNMENT

KRA beats its September customs target by KES 965 million

Customs collected KES 90.053 billion against a Treasury target of KES 89.088 billion, or 101.1%, up 5.8% on September 2025's KES 85.146 billion. Non-oil revenue was KES 57.199 billion, KES 1.778 billion ahead of target and about 64% of the month, while everything else came in KES 812 million short. The notable gap is between imports and revenue: total import value rose 25.6% and bulk cargo 49.2% — led by fertilisers, iron and steel, palm oil and rice — against 5.8% growth in customs revenue, which the article suggests may reflect lower duty rates or exemptions on bulk goods. KRA points to the Advance Cargo Declaration, data-led flagging of high-risk consignments and tighter action on illicit trade.

CLIMATE

PwC struggles to sell Koko's carbon credits, and creditors face steep losses

As administrator of Koko Networks (UK) Limited, PwC says the offers it has received for the carbon credit inventory — listed at about KES 47.2 million (£274,992) — are too low to give the estate "a meaningful return". It has spoken to three potential buyers and five brokers, engaged a broker, and as of its 17 September report had completed no sale. Unsecured claims total £127.17 million (about KES 21.83 billion) against estimated recoverable assets of £356,067 (about KES 61.1 million), and PwC expects no payout to unsecured creditors, though some employee claims may be covered by a separate funding arrangement. PwC says Kenya had not granted the approval needed to sell into compliance markets, which blocked premium-price sales — the same authorisation gap behind Koko's January shutdown. Tonnage, prices and bidders are not disclosed, and a joint sale of Koko's intellectual property is still being monitored.

SECURITY

Cyberattacks in Africa grew fivefold in a decade, a Mastercard report says

Mastercard's whitepaper Africa's Digital Defense Race, released on 12 October, puts the rise in African cyberattacks from 2016 to 2025 at 421%, against 307% globally — roughly in step with mobile money users (up 5.9 times), digital banking users (4.7 times) and e-commerce (5.3 times). Its own platform logged 69,236 cyber events against African organisations in 2025, about one every eight minutes; Nigeria, South Africa, Morocco and Kenya accounted for 54.7%, and over 60% of attributable actors were criminal groups. Kenya gets one sentence, with Mastercard's Shabir Ahmed saying mobile money "has become national infrastructure". Kenya's share and affected sectors are not in the public summary, and the full report sits behind a sign-up form. KE-CIRT's 2.36 billion detected threats in April–June 2026 count different things — about 96% are classed as system vulnerabilities — so the figures can't be compared. Mastercard has a commercial stake, having bought Recorded Future for US$2.65 billion; INTERPOL estimated Kenyans lost KES 491.6 million to SIM-swap fraud in 2025.
