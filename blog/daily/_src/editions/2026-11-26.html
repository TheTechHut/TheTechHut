Kenya Power needs engineers: 2,216 staff short, with 1,469 more retiring by 2030

Kenya Power has 11,203 employees against the 13,419 it says it needs to run its network, Techweez reports, and 1,469 of them are due to retire by 2030. In the five years to June 2024, 2,234 staff retired, faster than the company hired. Managing Director Joseph Siror has said engineers have been lost for years to retirement and study abroad, and that recent hiring has not made up for it. The utility is running a phased, three-year recruitment programme for its under-staffed departments. Its last graduate trainee advert asked for electrical and electronics, civil, mechanical, mechatronics and geospatial (GIS) engineers, and listed computer science and software engineering among the related fields it accepted. Openings and industrial attachments are posted on its careers portal, kplc.co.ke/careers, where you register before applying. Why it matters here: power distribution is going digital, with smart meters, GIS mapping and grid automation, and Kenya Power is one of the few employers hiring engineers at this scale. Check each advert's exact requirements, and never pay anyone who promises you a Kenya Power job.

Kenya and the EU agree a digital roadmap: AI money, fibre and freer data flows

The Digital Dialogue in Brussels on 23 and 24 November ended with a joint plan. The EU will put €10 million into Kenyan partnerships with its AI Factories, starting with language diversity and agriculture, and the two sides are exploring an open-access AI compute facility in Kenya for startups and researchers. Team Europe's pipeline includes 3,000km of aerial fibre and connections for 3,000 schools, health centres and hubs. Technical talks on mutual data adequacy are finished; once adopted, European firms could send personal data to Kenya for processing without extra safeguards, a boost for outsourcing and data jobs.

NCBA lent Sh1.3 trillion through phones in nine months

Digital loans, including M-Shwari and Fuliza with Safaricom, rose 27.2% to the end of September, more than Sh4.7 billion a day, and net profit rose 14.5% to Sh18.8 billion. These are the first results since Nedbank took a 66% stake on 3 November. NCBA also reports 99.7% uptime on its digital channels and launched Property Duka, which it calls an AI-native property platform.

Seven of the ten Latitude59 Kenya finalists are Kenyan

A record 509 startups from 25 countries applied, up from 222 last year. The Kenyan finalists are Inner Circle, Farmsky, Sustainable Africa, ElementPay, MasomoGPT, Chargebyte and Bahari Mezani, alongside Nigeria's Ruralpass and BetaLife and Uganda's Kyuka Ventures. They pitch in Nairobi on 4 December; the winner goes to Tallinn for Latitude59 2027.

Little drivers can now buy Absa insurance inside the app

The ride-hailing company and Absa Bank Kenya signed a bancassurance deal on Wednesday: personal accident, motor and endowment cover, with premiums, exclusions and claims steps shown before you buy. Kenya's insurance penetration reached 2.63% of GDP in 2025, and informal workers such as drivers are among the least covered.

FIFA confirms a cyberattack after a group claims 12GB of its data

FIFA said on 25 November that attackers reached a limited set of personal and business information. A group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club claims more than 12GB, including identity and financial documents; FIFA has not confirmed the volume. Reports suggest the attackers posed as FIFA staff, which FIFA has not confirmed. Like Todoist this week, it is a breach of people and access controls, not code.
