Microsoft's Outlook has been down for most of a day, and the company still has no full fix

The trouble started on Thursday evening Kenyan time, when users around the world found they could not open their Outlook mailboxes on the web, in the app or on their phones, and many emails arrived late. Teams and Copilot were disrupted too, though Teams chats and calls have largely kept working. Within an hour Downdetector had reports from 74 countries, and its count of Outlook problems peaked above 131,000, a figure that only includes people who bothered to file one. By Friday afternoon Kenyan time, roughly 19 to 20 hours in, Microsoft was reporting "broader improvements" in North America and "sustained improvements across much of Europe", while warning that problems may continue on and off. Earlier it had spoken of only "marginal success", with restored servers failing again. Microsoft has not published a root cause. It says many servers became "unexpectedly unavailable", which put heavy load on the rest, that the load was not caused by extra traffic, and that it was rolling back a network change. It says there is no indication of a cyberattack. Government systems in Singapore and Japan, the Supreme Court of the Philippines and Australia's federal police were among those affected. It is Microsoft's third big interruption this year, after a ten-hour Microsoft 365 failure in North America in January and Outlook lockouts in late August. If your employer runs on Microsoft 365, assume some mail from the past day is delayed, not lost, and confirm anything urgent, such as an interview time, by phone or WhatsApp.

Anthropic's IPO is open to ordinary investors in 15 countries, and Kenya isn't one of them

The maker of Claude plans to sell 825 million shares on Nasdaq at US$70 to US$78 each, raising about US$64 billion at the top of the range. Retail offers cover the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan and ten European countries. Kenyans can't apply, but anyone whose broker or app carries Nasdaq stocks can buy at the market price once trading starts, expected on 19 November from 5:30pm Kenyan time. That price may be above or below the IPO range.

A breach at AI provider Fireworks exposed chat transcripts from about 1% of Notion's customers

Attackers got into Fireworks AI through its cloud provider, its second breach in two months, and took billing details and some datasets. Notion says transcripts from customers who used its "Insights" AI feature were taken, cut its connection to Fireworks on 3 November and deleted the data held there. Anything you type into a work AI tool can end up stored at a company you have never heard of.

AI

United Airlines blocks AI assistants from booking flights for customers

The airline says handing your login to a third-party AI agent can expose personal data and lead to purchases or use of miles you did not intend, and that rules require a person to check in and confirm what is in their bags. Bank of America gave a similar warning about banking passwords last week. Companies are starting to push back on agents that act with a customer's own account.

You can now send DHL parcels from 60 Rubis petrol stations

DHL Express Kenya and Rubis Energy are turning stations in Nairobi, the Coast, Nyanza, Nakuru and Naivasha into DHL Service Points for documents and parcels going within Kenya or to more than 220 countries and territories. That is about one in five Rubis stations. Prices and opening hours haven't been published, and not every point takes international shipments, so check DHL's locator first.

Carrefour opens in Lavington with self-checkout lanes and app delivery

The store at ATZERO on James Gichuru Road created 45 jobs. Majid Al Futtaim, which runs Carrefour here, had 34 Kenyan stores at the end of June and plans nine more in the second half of the year. Its Kenyan revenue rose to about KES 48.8 billion last year, close to Quickmart's KSh50.4 billion.
