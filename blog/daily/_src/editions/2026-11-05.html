Safaricom's profit rises 36% to KSh 58.2 billion, and M-PESA passes KSh 100 billion in six months

Safaricom's net income rose 35.9% to KSh 58.2 billion in the six months to 30 September, on service revenue up 13.1% to KSh 226.1 billion. Kenya did the heavy lifting: its operating profit passed KSh 100 billion in a half-year for the first time, at KSh 101.8 billion, and M-PESA revenue rose 13.6% to KSh 100.1 billion, also a first. Mobile data revenue grew 12.2% to KSh 45.2 billion, with the average customer using 5.3GB a month. The fastest growth is in lending. Fuliza, the M-PESA overdraft run with NCBA and KCB, earned KSh 4.9 billion, up 86%, as borrowers nearly doubled to 17.2 million and it lent out KSh 1.16 trillion. The average Fuliza loan fell 24.6% to KSh 192, and repayments came to 99.6% of what was lent, down from 102.4% a year earlier, so more people are borrowing smaller sums and slightly less is coming back. KCB M-PESA and M-Shwari both lost active customers. Ethiopia is getting closer to paying its way: revenue rose 71.6% to KSh 10.6 billion with 15.4 million customers, and its EBITDA loss shrank 96.5% to KSh 434 million, though it still lost KSh 8.8 billion after depreciation and interest. Safaricom kept its full-year guidance of KSh 180 billion to KSh 187 billion in group operating profit, and still aims for EBITDA breakeven in Ethiopia by the end of its financial year in March 2027. Net debt rose to KSh 193.4 billion from KSh 140.9 billion.

EDUCATION

KNEC's online teacher exams face questions after a reported failure at Kigari

KNEC now runs the teacher training diplomas entirely online, on its TECBA e-assessment system. On 4 November, trainees at St. Mark's Teachers College, Kigari, reportedly lost access mid-way through a three-hour paper, some could not finish, and police were deployed after protests. KNEC has not published an account of what happened. Its instructions allow no extra time and do not say what happens when the platform or a college's servers fail, and a supplementary sitting costs KES 7,200.

ELECTIONS

The CA asks the IEBC to map polling stations without 3G or 4G before 2027

The regulator has asked operators for coverage data down to sub-location level and the IEBC for each polling station's coordinates, so it can find where results cannot be sent over mobile data and satellite links are needed. In 2022 the IEBC used satellite at 1,111 polling centres. Bids for a three-year satellite contract closed on 30 October, with no winner named, and the IEBC plans to publish the list of stations without coverage in July 2027, ahead of the 10 August election.

AI

Kenya comes 22nd of 56 in a Commonwealth index of young people's AI readiness

The Ayre Think Tank's index, which looks at access, skills, work opportunities and protections for people aged 15 to 29, scored Kenya 48.7 out of 100, short of its 53-point benchmark, which 15 countries met, including Nigeria and South Africa. Uganda came 23rd, Tanzania 25th and Rwanda 47th. Data for Kenya covered 66 of the 102 indicators, so treat the exact rank with some caution.

BANKING

Postbank agents can now take cash deposits for other banks' customers, over PesaLink

Customers of participating banks will be able to deposit cash into their own accounts at more than 600 Postbank branches and agents, with PesaLink carrying the money across. It covers deposits only, not withdrawals. The partners have not named the first banks or SACCOs, the fees, the limits or how fast the money arrives, so ask your bank before you go.

FINTECH

A Chinese regulator's inquiry is behind OPay's halted US$4 billion listing, Bloomberg reports

OPay priced 16 million shares at US$19 each, valuing it at about US$4 billion, then stopped its New York listing on 30 October over an inquiry from what it called an immaterial jurisdiction. Bloomberg reports the inquiry came from a Chinese regulator. OPay's prospectus said it did not need China's approval under its 2023 overseas listing rules, as it earns no revenue in mainland China. Nigeria brings in about 88% of its revenue, and its founder Zhou Yahui is Chinese.
