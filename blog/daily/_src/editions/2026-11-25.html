Africa bought 40% fewer smartphones this quarter, and the cheapest ones vanished fastest

Smartphone shipments to Africa fell to 13.7 million in the third quarter of 2026, from 22.8 million a year earlier, according to research firm Omdia. That ends three years of third-quarter growth, and the steepest losses were in phones under US$100. The average selling price hit US$222, the highest for a third quarter in a decade, while feature phone shipments rose 27%: people who cannot afford an upgrade are keeping or going back to basic phones. Kenya's shipments fell 35%, less than Nigeria's 47% and Egypt's 41% but more than South Africa's 20%, where contract upgrades and operator sales cushion the blow. TechTrends notes that shipments are new devices, not the phones already in use. Why it matters here: if you build apps for Kenyans, the phone in your user's hand is getting older, not newer. Test on low-memory Android phones, keep downloads small, and keep USSD and SMS options for users who are back on feature phones.

The Hustler Fund gets KES 5 billion more, and Bridge Loans of up to KES 150,000 reach more borrowers

President Ruto announced the extra money on 24 November at State House, and said Bridge Loans would open to 4.5 million regular borrowers. The fund has lent KES 98 billion to more than 10 million borrowers as of October, Techweez reports, though its budget had fallen from KES 5 billion in 2024/25 to KES 300 million in 2025/26. He also asked for a KES 300 million product for organised groups. Neither the source of the money nor a start date was given, and limits still depend on how you have repaid.

tele.ke draws a KSh1.33 million bid in KeNIC's premium domain auction

More than 620 short .ke names, registered straight under .ke rather than .co.ke, are up for auction until 4 December. motorbike.ke is at KSh1.16 million, and derma.ke and smoothie.ke are above KSh910,000; KeNIC says buyers in East Asia, especially China, want two- and three-letter names. Kenya now has about 147,000 .ke domains, up from 111,268 in June 2025. If you build websites for small businesses, a plain .ke name is now an option to offer them, at normal prices outside the auction.

AI

The Central Bank wants its economists to learn AI

Governor Kamau Thugge said on 24 November that CBK will train staff to use AI and machine learning in analysing the economy, including tracking activity between official statistics releases. In October its research director said the bank had found nearly 300 possible AI uses across 14 departments. For data scientists, it is another sign that Kenyan employers want people who can explain where a model's answer is unreliable, not just run it.

Botswana's BTC plans to connect ordinary 4G phones straight to Starlink satellites

The direct-to-device service is due to launch commercially in early 2027, starting with SMS, maps, WhatsApp calls and light browsing, aimed at tourism, mining, farming and wildlife areas beyond the towers. No price has been given. In Kenya, Airtel and SpaceX completed testing of the same kind of service in March under a deal covering 14 Airtel markets.

SECURITY

Todoist confirms a breach of about 6% of its customers, through stolen staff credentials

The to-do app says an attacker used stolen credentials to reach its source code on 22 and 23 November, then an employee's work account to get into its cloud systems, taking email addresses, password hashes, API tokens and Slack integration tokens. Passwords have been reset. If you use Todoist, change any password you reused elsewhere and reconnect its integrations.
