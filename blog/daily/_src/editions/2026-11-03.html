KRA gives Google, Microsoft and other giants until 17 November to sign up for Kenya's 15% minimum tax

The Kenya Revenue Authority has set 17 November as the deadline for large multinational groups to notify it that they fall under Kenya's new minimum top-up tax. The rules cover groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million, about KES 109 billion, in at least two of the previous four years. If such a group's effective tax rate on the profits it earns in Kenya comes out below 15%, it may owe a top-up to bring it to 15%. TechCabal lists Google, Microsoft, Coca-Cola, Nestlé, Unilever, DHL and TotalEnergies among the groups that could be caught, if they meet the conditions. This is Kenya's version of the OECD's global minimum tax, which is meant to stop large companies booking profits in low-tax countries while doing business elsewhere. The 60-day notification window opened in September. A notification must name the Kenyan company, its group, the group's ultimate parent, its financial year and the other group members operating in Kenya. Filing returns and paying any top-up have separate, later deadlines. For the Treasury it is a way to raise revenue from very large companies instead of borrowing more. What it will actually raise is not known yet: KRA has not published an estimate, and how much each company pays depends on how its profits and Kenyan taxes are counted under the new rules.

TRADE

The US drops South Africa and Rwanda from AGOA from 1 January 2027

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said both countries engaged in practices inconsistent with AGOA's rules, including barriers to American trade and investment. They can still export to the US, but without AGOA's duty-free treatment. South Africa exported US$1.6 billion of goods under AGOA in 2025, and cars and car parts made up 64% of its AGOA-eligible exports in 2024, though AGOA goods were only about 10% of what it sells to the US. Rwanda's exposure is small. Congress extended AGOA to the end of 2028 in September, but each country is still reviewed every year. The announcement named only South Africa and Rwanda.

Anthropic starts watermarking text from more Claude models today

From 3 November, writing from Claude Mythos 5, Claude Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 carries an invisible watermark, a pattern in which words the model picks, guided by a secret key. Newer models such as Claude Opus 5.5 already had it. It adds no characters, does not change quality and carries nothing that identifies the user. Anthropic says it is complying with the EU AI Act and applies it worldwide. Only Anthropic's detector can check for it, in a private preview for regulators, media, fact-checkers and researchers. Short or lightly edited text is hard to judge, and a full rewrite removes the mark.

GOVTECH

The Post Office gets six refrigerated trucks to carry vaccines and insulin

The Communications Authority's Post4Health project gives Postal Corporation of Kenya six cold-chain trucks, about KES 65 million in all from the Universal Service Fund and the Universal Postal Union, to carry temperature-sensitive medicines to remote health facilities. Post Office parcels fell 33% to about 993,000 in the quarter to June, while private couriers grew 9% to 14.4 million. New postal and courier regulations, covering licensing, compensation and data protection for delivery apps, could be in force by the end of the year.

FINTECH

MTN moves MoMo onto Alipay+ as its Nigerian fintech revenue falls 26%

MTN Nigeria's fintech revenue fell 25.9% to ₦97.5 billion in the nine months to September, after it paused its airtime and data credit service from April to meet new lending rules. MoMo moved to Ant International's Alipay+ platform in August, and MTN reports 3.2 million monthly active MoMo users, against about 50 million active customers at OPay. Core mobile money revenue doubled as wallet balances quadrupled to ₦43.8 billion.

FUNDING

BII plans to put US$3 billion into Africa's riskiest markets over five years

British International Investment, the UK government's development investor, plans about £2.25 billion, roughly US$3 billion, for frontier economies such as Sierra Leone, Liberia, Ethiopia and the DRC, according to Bloomberg. It is about a quarter of the £9 billion BII expects to invest and mobilise across Africa under its 2026 to 2031 strategy. BII invests for returns, in companies, banks, infrastructure and funds, and hopes its early bets bring pension funds and insurers in behind it.
