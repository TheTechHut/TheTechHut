Airtel Money prices in London at $7bn — and shuts African retail investors out

The offer is priced at $2.60 (£1.96) a share, valuing the mobile money arm at roughly $7 billion. Existing shareholders are selling 270 million shares for about $700 million, rising to around $770 million if the over-allotment is taken up, leaving a free float of 16.5% — 17.5% fully exercised — enough for FTSE UK index inclusion. Every share on offer is existing stock, so Airtel Money itself raises nothing; the proceeds go to the sellers. Conditional dealings were expected by 9 October with unconditional trading from 14 October. The line that should land hardest in Nairobi: the retail offer is not open to investors in Kenya or other African markets. A business whose subscribers, agents and transaction volumes are overwhelmingly African is being sold to institutions in London, and the people who built the float cannot participate.

M-PESA arrives at the till as a tap

Safaricom and Pesapal have put two things on Pesapal's point-of-sale terminals: tap-to-pay, where an NFC phone touches the device and the customer confirms, and dynamic scan-to-pay, where the QR code on the terminal carries the merchant details and the amount so nobody mistypes either. Scan-to-pay runs at over 60,000 merchant locations today and Safaricom wants 500,000 within twelve months — more than eightfold. "This means moving beyond simply enabling payments," says group CTO James Maitai; Pesapal founder Agosta Liko calls it the biggest leap in Kenyan checkout in years. The context nobody says out loud: physical retail tap has been card territory, and this is M-PESA taking it. Note too who Pesapal's new shareholder is — KCB, 22.23%, as Tanzania's regulator disclosed last week.

Bolt Send passes a million deliveries, and Kenya is its fastest-growing market

A million parcels in under a year since the September 2025 relaunch, with order volumes up more than 135% between January and July 2026 and motorcycle deliveries up over 130% — enough to overtake Ghana on two-wheel volumes in May. Car delivery for larger parcels launched in March, and Mombasa became the first market outside Nairobi in August, though Nairobi still accounted for over 95% of volume through July. The operational texture: 9.4km average trip, couriers rated 4.84, Fridays running 23% above the daily average. The structural advantage is regulatory — Bolt is the only app-based delivery platform holding a National Courier Operator licence from the CA, gazetted in August 2025 and fully licensed in July 2026.
