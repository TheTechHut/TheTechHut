Ruto nominates Lydia Sitienei Chelagat as Kenya's next Data Commissioner

Government Spokesperson Charles Owino Wahong'o announced the nomination on Monday 9 November, saying the President acted on the recommendation of the Public Service Commission. Her name now goes to the National Assembly for consideration under the Data Protection Act, so she is not yet in office. The recruitment drew 152 applications; the PSC shortlisted 13 candidates and scheduled interviews for 5 October. Chelagat would succeed Immaculate Kassait, who set up the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner and led it for six years. At Kassait's farewell dinner on 5 November, Dorothy Jemator of the Commission on Administrative Justice said she leaves an institution with its own identity and a growing body of rulings and guidance, and that the two bodies are working on joint advice where access to information and privacy meet. The office matters to anyone who builds or uses digital products in Kenya: it decides complaints about how companies and public bodies collect, store and share personal data, and it has built an enforcement record across sectors that handle sensitive information. Whoever leads it next inherits the questions that have arrived since, including how the law applies to AI systems.

Equity is building an AI tool to predict which employees are about to leave

The group's 2025 sustainability report says it is developing "predictive analytics to forecast attrition and identify risk windows", so managers can act before specialists go. Banks, telcos and fintechs are competing for the same digital and payments talent. Equity's staff turnover was 10.6% last year, it hired 3,198 people, and staff costs rose 18% to KES 39.5 billion. If you have those skills, employers are now measuring how likely you are to be poached.

PesaLink now connects all 38 commercial banks and moves KES 4 billion a day

The bank-owned network has 80 member institutions after adding 12 in a year, and handles about KES 110 billion a month, up 41%, with roughly 98% of transactions succeeding. TechCabal's analysis is that cheaper transfers alone won't dislodge M-PESA, which has more than 260,000 agents and is what most shops and billers already accept.

LOOP's new counter device takes cards, M-PESA and LOOP payments on one terminal

The LOOP Biz PDQ accepts Visa and Mastercard by chip or tap, M-PESA Till, Paybill and STK push, and LOOP STK push, which is free between LOOP users. It is tied to the merchant's LOOP Till, and takings can be settled to a bank account of the merchant's choice.

Samsung plans paid Galaxy AI tiers, with the core features staying free

The proposed Starter, Plus and Pro plans come with 10,000, 20,000 and 50,000 credits to spend on heavier features such as turning photos into short videos and automating actions across apps, once free allowances run out. One-off credit packs would last three years. Samsung has not announced prices or a launch date.

Africa has 0.6% of the world's data centre capacity and should build its own for AI, an industry body says

TICON Africa president David Gowu says last week's sell-off in US AI and data centre shares shows the risk of depending on infrastructure elsewhere; UBS estimates about 30% of US capacity planned for 2028 could be at risk. A 2025 Access Partnership report projects a 98% gap between AI computing demand and data centre capacity in sub-Saharan Africa by 2030.
