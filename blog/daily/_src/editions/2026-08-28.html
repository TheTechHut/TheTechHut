CA brings Uber, Bolt and Glovo parcel deliveries under licence

From 20 September 2026, app-based courier operators must verify sender and recipient identities, record declared parcel contents, offer real-time tracking, and keep delivery records retrievable by the Communications Authority, KRA and police. Licences cost Sh5,000 to apply and Sh100,000 initially, with an annual fee of Sh100,000 or 0.4% of gross turnover — whichever is higher — plus a 0.5% universal service levy. Platforms must compensate for lost or damaged goods within 90 days. Uber applied for a national courier licence in June; Bolt is running a rider licensing programme with Mombasa County.

Kenya's power reserve margin collapses to 3.34%, reviving rationing fears

KNBS data shows the buffer above peak demand fell from 20.73% in January to 3.34% in June, against a comfortable 20–35% norm. Peak demand hit 2,549MW in July and is projected at 2,680MW by 2027 — leaving data centres and heavy industry exposed to any plant or transmission failure.

Kenya moves closer to joining the Budapest Convention on cybercrime

Invited to accede in October 2024 and approved by Cabinet in February 2026, the treaty now awaits parliamentary endorsement. It would open 24-hour channels for cross-border evidence requests — and raises questions about foreign access to Kenyan subscriber and traffic data under the Data Protection Act.
