Nigeria's stock exchange group buys 10% of the Nairobi Securities Exchange

The Nigerian Exchange Group (NGX Group) has taken a 10% equity stake in the Nairobi Securities Exchange, its second East African bet after an earlier investment in the Ethiopian Securities Exchange. Neither side has disclosed the price, the completion date or any regulatory conditions. NGX says the stake gives it exposure to one of East Africa's more established capital markets, spreads its holdings beyond Nigeria, and positions it for growth as African markets link up. “Integration is moving from aspiration to architecture,” said NGX Group chairman Umaru Kwairanga, while group CEO Temi Popoola called Kenya “a cornerstone of East Africa's financial ecosystem”. The NSE is one of the few African exchanges listed on its own board. The deal comes during a busy few weeks for Nairobi's market: Quickmart opened 33% above its IPO price last week, and the CMA now licenses crypto service providers alongside the CBK. Why it matters here: closer ties between Lagos and Nairobi could eventually make it easier for Kenyans to buy Nigerian-listed shares, and for Nigerian money to reach Kenyan companies, but nothing in this deal changes how you trade today.

Nigeria's Payaza gets a CBK licence to process payments for Kenyan merchants

The licence, issued on 5 November, covers online checkout and accepting card, mobile money and bank payments; it does not allow deposit-taking or customer wallets. Payaza says it works in more than 27 countries and 40 currencies, and got a similar licence in Zambia 17 days earlier. More gateways means more choice, and more price competition, for Kenyan online sellers.

Netflix now lets Kenyans add someone outside the home for KSh250 a month

The option appeared on Netflix's Kenyan pricing page without an announcement, three years after the company said Kenya would not get it. Standard subscribers (KSh700) can add one extra member and Premium (KSh1,100) up to two, each with their own login, one screen and the plan's video quality. The extra member must live in Kenya, and Basic and Mobile plans are excluded. Netflix has not said whether it will block shared logins.

SECURITY

Safaricom says it now treats security as part of every product launch, as AI scams rise

Products must pass security, safety and privacy reviews before launch, and M-PESA now shows less of your personal information to the person you send money to, starting with person-to-person transfers. Safaricom warns that fake festive promotions will rise: you cannot win a promotion you did not enter, and it calls customers from one official number only.

PAYMENTS

Cheap African cross-border payments are here, but banks' markups eat the savings, says PAPSS

The Pan-African Payment and Settlement System charges about 0.1% to settle payments in local currencies and now runs in 31 countries, linked to Kenya through Pesalink since February. Its CEO, Mike Ogbalu, says some partner banks add their own fees, so businesses do not always see the lower cost. PAPSS aims to reach 37 countries by the end of the year.

Kenya and the EU move closer to a data adequacy deal

ICT Principal Secretary John Tanui said technical talks are expected to conclude positively, though no date was given. An adequacy decision would let personal data flow between the EU and Kenya with fewer legal hurdles, which matters for outsourcing, cloud and software firms serving European clients. The two sides also discussed AI, digital ID, cybersecurity and broadband.
