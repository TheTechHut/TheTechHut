Nvidia won't bring back Jensen Huang, reports US$115.8 billion in revenue, and stops selling H200 chips to China

On its first earnings call since Huang resigned, Nvidia's chairman Harvey Jones said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”, a day after Huang posted that he would be “honored” to return if asked and after more than 115,000 people signed a petition for him. Interim CEO Colette Kress, who presented the results, called it “business as usual”. The numbers back her up for now: revenue for the quarter was US$115.8 billion, up 103% on a year earlier and above Nvidia's own US$108 billion forecast, and it expects about US$130 billion next quarter. The shares, which fell about 10% after Huang's exit, rose about 4% after hours. The bigger change is China. Kress said Nvidia will no longer sell H200 chips there and is ending its part in the arrangement that let them be shipped under a 25% US tariff. The board also set up a committee for national security and export controls. New US rules bar advanced chips from foreign data centres that serve Chinese customers remotely; Jefferies estimates they could cut US$44 billion to US$62 billion from Nvidia's data centre revenue next year, though Kress says the backlog will cover it. The SEC is reported to be investigating the company, and the board's own inquiry continues. Why it matters here: Nvidia's chips sit under most of the AI tools Kenyans now pay for, and data centres in Africa that serve Chinese clients may now face the same rules.

MARKETS

The NSE will stay open until 4pm from 1 January, its first change in trading time since 2008

Equities, bonds and derivatives will trade for seven hours instead of six, so Nairobi overlaps more with trading in Europe, the US and West Africa. The exchange had 2.36 million investors in September, up from 2.03 million in January, and market value reached KSh4.12 trillion. A single KSh204 billion Safaricom block trade accounts for about half of this year's equity turnover, so not all of that activity is everyday trading.

More than 100 former Meta moderators in Nairobi have been diagnosed with PTSD, depression or anxiety, their lawyer's group says

The Oversight Lab wants Meta to compensate them and Parliament to drop a clause in the Business Laws (Amendment) Bill that it says would shield tech clients from outsourcing claims; the Senate had the amendments on its order paper today. The moderators' case, joined by 185 people, has no trial date. A letter dated 6 November says Sama, which employed them, is winding down with about US$1 million in assets against US$17 million in liabilities. Meta had not responded publicly.

PRIVACY

Kenya's company registry pays no fine for leaking two million firms' records

Data from the Business Registration Service, including about 2.09 million addresses, 830,000 email addresses and beneficial ownership details, appeared on a foreign company-data site in 2025. The Data Commissioner issued an enforcement notice instead of a penalty, and BRS carried out the fixes. Under the law a fine follows only if a notice is ignored, and no one filed a complaint, so no one has been compensated.

ENERGY

A Greek company wants to moor a floating gas power plant in Mombasa to power AI data centres

AMACO Energy signed an agreement on 6 November with ARISE IIP Kenya to study a 250-megawatt plant on a barge in the Dongo Kundu economic zone, expandable to 1,000 MW, at a cost of at least US$700 million. The government did not sign, no gas supplier or data centre customer has been named, and Kenya has no LNG import terminal yet. Construction is targeted for 2027.

WORLD

An Apple update knocks out iCloud sign-in, the App Store and Apple Music for hours

Apple's status page listed the iCloud sign-in problem as affecting “all users”; some people could not activate new iPhones. Apple said “an internal update caused some outages” and that it was not a cyberattack. Everything was marked fixed by the evening, US time. It is the third big tech outage in a week, after Microsoft's and the US air traffic network's.
