KRA's chairman revives the 25% tax on phones when they first connect, which Parliament threw out

KRA board chairman Ndiritu Muriithi has defended a proposal to charge excise duty of 25% on a phone's value when it is first activated on a Kenyan network, an idea that was in this year's Finance Bill until Parliament's Finance Committee recommended removing it and it was dropped from the Finance Act. Today phones pay 10% excise when they are imported or made locally, and that has not changed. Speaking in Laikipia, Muriithi said import checks are being "beaten by under-declaration and the misuse of consolidated cargo", where several traders share a container and high-value phones are hidden among cheap ones. In one case reported by Business Daily in September, investigators found 55,607 basic smartphones in a shipment declaring 3,000, plus 309 undeclared high-end phones. A tax charged at activation would give KRA a second checkpoint at the network, and the Treasury argued honest importers would pay later instead of tying up cash in stock. The objections that sank it have not gone away. The Bill never defined "activation": switching on, inserting a SIM, or first connecting. Second-hand buyers could not tell whether a phone had been taxed. It would probably need IMEI tracking, and the High Court quashed KRA and CA directives demanding IMEI data in 2025. And the cost would land on buyers who depend on a phone for M-PESA and work. KRA has not published the size of the revenue gap. Meanwhile it is tightening the border instead: an October notice requires importers, agents and consolidators to disclose each importer and the value of goods in shared shipments, and since Sunday, imports without a Kenyan digital marine insurance certificate are being held at customs. There is no new phone tax in force, but the idea may come back when the next Finance Bill is drafted.

Airtel pays KES 8.4 billion for 100MHz of 5G spectrum for 25 years

The CA has approved Airtel Kenya's purchase of 100MHz in the 3.5GHz band, the main 5G band, for US$65 million, about KES 8.4 billion, for 25 years. Airtel says it will add capacity for 5G phones and home internet over 5G, where it is chasing Safaricom, which holds about 64% of mobile broadband. No coverage targets were given. The deal lands while the CA's new way of charging for spectrum is suspended by a tribunal and challenged by Jamii, with Safaricom now in the case.

CRYPTO

The FBI blames North Korea for the Binance theft, and withdrawals start to reopen

The FBI says North Korea's TraderTraitor group stole more than US$6 billion from Binance, about four times its 2025 Bybit theft. Bitcoin withdrawals restarted on Sunday morning, followed by Ether, USDT, USDC and BNB, with other coins to follow in phases. Binance says it has covered 95% of the loss from its treasury, its SAFU fund and part of its futures insurance fund, and is buying the rest; about US$3 billion is frozen, but trackers say a similar amount, including 18,270 Bitcoin, is still with the attacker. A full proof of reserves is now due on Tuesday. Peer-to-peer trading for M-PESA carried on through the pause, and Kenya's crypto licensing deadline is this Wednesday.

Google opens a free AI skills hub and aims to train 3 million Africans by 2030

Skilling Africa, launched today at skillingafrica.withgoogle.com, brings more than 15 Google programmes, including Hustle Academy, Career Certificates and Google Cloud training, into one free site for students, developers, founders and professionals. Lessons are short and light on data, can be downloaded on Wi-Fi and finished offline, and come with an AI study companion and badges for streaks. Sign-in is with a Google account and there is no app to install. Google did not give Kenyan targets or say which languages are covered.

CAREERS

DCI arrests an Eldoret agency director over a KES 15 million overseas jobs scam

Detectives have arrested Isaac Kipkemei Barmasai, sole director of Proline Travel Advisors, after 29 people said they paid KES 200,000 to KES 1 million each in 2023 and 2024 for jobs in Sweden, Australia, the UK and Canada that did not exist. He has been charged with obtaining money by false pretences, and the case returns on 4 November. Before paying any recruitment fee, check that the agency is licensed with the National Employment Authority and that the employer and job are real.

GADGETS

TECNO and Infinix's parent sees revenue fall 27% as memory prices bite

Transsion, which makes TECNO, Infinix and itel phones, reported third-quarter revenue of 14.85 billion yuan, down 27%, and its shipments in Africa fell 39%, its worst region. Memory chips have become more expensive as manufacturers move capacity to AI data centres, and Transsion has been passing that on: its average smartphone price rose to about KES 13,900 in the first half from about KES 10,700 a year earlier. Budget phones are getting dearer whether or not KRA gets a new tax.
