The US grounded every departing flight for more than four hours. The government blames a routine cable upgrade

On Friday afternoon US time, the Federal Aviation Administration ordered all departing traffic across the United States to stay on the ground “until further notice”, citing “extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety”. It was only the third nationwide ground stop this century. The FAA pointed to communication problems at several of its regional air traffic control centres, and the stop was lifted after more than four hours. The data firm Cirium counted more than 8,200 flights cancelled on Friday evening and at least 3,000 more on Saturday. On Saturday, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the trigger was “routine maintenance”: a contractor, L3Harris, was replacing copper wire with fibre-optic cable at a remote site when a router overloaded a critical communication system. The FAA's acting technology chief said two sources had been flooding the network with about a thousand connections every four to five seconds before they were shut off. Officials are still working out whether the cause was a user, the installer or a faulty router, and the FAA administrator said there was “zero evidence of anything nefarious”. The outage came the same week the FAA introduced an AI tool for air traffic controllers, which officials say played no part. Why it matters here: anyone flying to or through the US this week should expect knock-on delays, and it is a reminder that one small change in a shared network can stop an entire system.

SECURITY

Uber says hackers took drivers' licence details through its AI provider, Fireworks

The attackers broke into Fireworks AI in October, stole internal credentials and used them to reach Uber's data. Uber has not said how many drivers are affected; notices filed in three US states alone cover 7,870 people. It is the same breach that exposed Notion customers' chat transcripts last week. Every outside AI tool a company plugs in is another way in.

More than 115,000 people, Elon Musk among them, petition Nvidia to bring back Jensen Huang

The petition says Huang was removed “without meaningful due process” and asks the board to reinstate him while an independent investigation runs. Replit's Amjad Masad, Epic Games' Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator's Garry Tan also signed. The site lists nearly 6,000 current Nvidia employees among the signatories. Nvidia has not responded; Colette Kress remains interim CEO.

Kenya's home broadband latency is second-lowest among 16 English-speaking countries

User speed tests collected by nPerf from November 2025 to October 2026 put Kenya at 29 milliseconds, behind only New Zealand and ahead of the UK, the US, South Africa and Nigeria, and 22% better than a year earlier. Fixed internet subscriptions reached 2.84 million in June, up 32%. The figures are averages of user tests, not a controlled measure of network quality.

Tesla opens Cybercab pre-orders at US$29,990, plus US$299 a month to run it

Select US customers can order the two-seat robotaxi for personal use, with deliveries promised from December in a handful of Texas cities. It will only drive itself inside geofenced areas where Tesla runs its robotaxi service. Elon Musk said the monthly fee was “too high”.
