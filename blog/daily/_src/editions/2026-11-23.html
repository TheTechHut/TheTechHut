A former Microsoft engineer builds Ajira AI to find you jobs and tailor your CV for each one

Ajira AI puts job discovery, CV tailoring, cover letters and application autofill in one place. Its founder, Gideon Kiratu, spent 14 years as a software engineer and was a senior engineer at Microsoft. He started building in October and opened it to the public on 10 November. It reads about 28 job sources, including Fuzu in Kenya and remote boards such as Jobicy and Himalayas, and has testers in Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda and Malawi. Matching works in two passes: first direct keyword matches, then AI to judge related experience, giving a match score and the skills you are missing. A Chrome extension fills in forms on sites such as Workable but never submits for you. There is a free tier with 10 fit analyses a month, a seven-day Job Sprint pass and monthly or quarterly plans. In its first ten days it had about 100 sign-ups and 47 active users. Kiratu says it is paid for by subscriptions, sells no personal data and does not train AI models on candidates' details. Why it matters here: tailoring each application is what gets a CV past screening software, and it takes hours by hand. Whatever tool you use, read every line before you send it: an AI-written CV that claims skills you do not have will not survive the interview.

Safaricom takes Family Share, home fibre plus shared mobile bundles, to Mombasa

After Nairobi and Kisumu, Mombasa is the third city to get the plan, built with Huawei, which links one Home Fibre subscription to up to five family members' phones. The entry plan costs KES 3,749 a month for 40Mbps fibre plus either 750 minutes or 14GB of mobile data shared across the family. New and existing Home Fibre customers can join through My OneApp, *400# or a Safaricom shop. Zuku, Faiba and Airtel are the competition.

Samsung and Watu's TV financing costs about half as much again as paying cash

Buyers can now take a Samsung TV home for a KSh7,500 deposit and pay Watu daily or weekly over 12 months. TechTrends worked out the totals: the 32-inch set costs KSh25,190 in cash but KSh38,076 on the weekly plan, and across eight models financing adds 49% to 52%. Watu has financed more than 1,000 Samsung TVs so far. If you can save for a few months instead, you keep that extra half.

OpenAI's chief scientist proposes one number to cap AI development

Jakub Pachocki argues that benchmarks go stale too fast and that perplexity, how well a model predicts the next word, should be the shared measure of general intelligence, with companies agreeing to stop at a threshold until safety monitoring catches up. He notes that losses below 0.8 coincided with big jumps in cybersecurity and maths ability. Sam Altman called it the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while.

Signal blocks screenshots on phones, citing AI agents built into operating systems

Signal is extending to Android and iOS the screenshot block it already uses on Windows. Its president, Meredith Whittaker, calls operating systems whose AI agents can read the screen an existential threat to private messaging. Users can switch the block off, but she says that breaks Signal's privacy promise, and that the step is incomplete.

Nothing will show its first screenless AI device on 8 December

Carl Pei's company has teased a round metal device with a red record button; tech-ish suspects a recorder that turns conversations into notes, which Nothing has not confirmed. There is no name or price yet. Nothing sells officially in Kenya through Mitsumi Distribution.
