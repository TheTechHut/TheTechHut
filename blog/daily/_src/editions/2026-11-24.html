A hacker group says it breached eCitizen. Nobody has confirmed it yet

A threat actor calling itself ByteToBreach claims to have taken about 740 GB from eCitizen, the government services portal most Kenyans use for everything from passports to business names. The claim was shared on X by VECERT, a threat-intelligence firm in Miami. It says the haul is about 600 GB of KYC documents, 100 GB of database content and 40 GB of source code, taken through a weakness in a file-upload feature and a web shell, possibly reaching eCitizen's single sign-on as well. The allegations also name the Social Health Authority, the DCI, Safaricom and the National Police Service. None of it is verified: as Techweez notes, the Ministry of ICT, the national computer incident response team (KE-CIRT/CC) and the Data Protection Commissioner had not confirmed a breach. The backdrop is worrying, though. The Communications Authority counted 27.4 million web application attacks in its latest report, up 57.62%, and the State House website was breached in July. What to do now: watch for messages that quote your ID number or KRA PIN and ask you to "verify" or pay; criminals use leak claims, real or not, as bait. Why it matters here: if you build or secure public-facing systems, file uploads and single sign-on are exactly where security teams are hiring.

Kenya Pipeline's free tech fellowship says 27 of its first 45 graduates have jobs

The first Inuka Tech Fellowship, run by KPC Foundation, graduated 45 people on 19 November from more than 1,000 applicants; half the class are women. Thirty took a three-month data and AI track with Power Learn Project (Python, machine learning, dashboards and automation) and 15 trained for six months as fibre optic technicians with JHUB Africa at JKUAT. Five graduates are in talks with Family Bank about a tool they built. tech-ish notes the foundation has not said what counts as a job, and Power Learn Project put the figure at 30% in September. The next intake is due to be announced in the coming weeks.

E-COMMERCE

A "Sell on TikTok Shop" link shows up in Kenya, but the shop has not launched

tech-ish found the option in TikTok's menu on a computer in Kenya, pointing to a seller-ke address that did not exist when they checked on 23 November. TikTok has not announced TikTok Shop here, and Kenya is not among its 24 seller countries; the Netherlands, Poland and Belgium got the shop about two months after links appeared there. In the UK, TikTok takes 9% of a sale, which would be KES 180 on a KES 2,000 item. Until TikTok says otherwise, do not pay anyone offering you a "Kenyan TikTok Shop account".

FINTECH

Apis Partners buys control of Korridor, which lets truckers pay tolls and border fees digitally

The private equity firm has taken a majority stake from its US$1.23 billion Growth Fund III, for an undisclosed sum. Korridor, founded in 2007 and based in Mauritius, has more than 360 network points in 13 countries, over 2,200 customers and nearly 50,000 trucks on its network, paying tolls, border fees, fuel, insurance and parking. Apis plans to add credit and insurance for fleet owners and move more of the network onto a digital platform, which means product and engineering hiring for African road freight.

AI CHIPS

Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia's chief executive, 11 days after he resigned

Nvidia's board has reinstated its co-founder, and tech-ish reports the company's value rose by about US$1 trillion the morning he returned. The scandal around Megaspeed, the company at its centre, is not over: TechCrunch reports Megaspeed appears headed for a US blacklist and that Huang is due to testify before the Senate next week. Nvidia's chips run most of the world's AI training, so its leadership matters to every team renting GPUs.
