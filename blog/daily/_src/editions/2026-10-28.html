Safaricom wins a place in Jamii's fight over how the CA charges for spectrum

The High Court has ruled that Safaricom can join a case brought by Jamii Telecommunications, the smaller operator behind Faiba, against the Communications Authority's new spectrum fees, overturning a tribunal that had shut it out. In March 2025 the CA told licensees that from 1 July 2025 annual spectrum fees would no longer depend on how many transmitters an operator runs but on how much spectrum it holds, with a separate price for each band. Jamii argues the new schedule favours the biggest networks, is discriminatory and unaffordable, and was introduced after only superficial consultation; in April 2025 the Communications and Multimedia Appeals Tribunal suspended it while the appeal is heard, and it remains suspended. Safaricom asked to join, saying it pays the fees and that the outcome affects its costs and plans. The tribunal refused because it had not shown harm or said which side it backed, and the CA warned of "litigation by ambush". Justice John Anuro disagreed: Safaricom's filing was "a statement of prejudice" that "may not have been elaborate, but it is certainly not absent", and an interested party does not have to pick a side. He limited Safaricom to the issues Jamii raised, so it cannot add claims or take over the case. Nothing has been decided about whether the fees are fair. The amounts in dispute have not been published, but they are not small: Safaricom paid the CA KES 16.3 billion in licence fees in the year to March. A draft CA and ICT Authority market review warns that the new structure could raise costs for smaller networks and says pricing should not push them out, which is close to Jamii's argument. The tribunal will now set dates for Safaricom's evidence.

AI

China's AI stocks lose about US$480 billion in under four months

Shanghai's STAR 50 index, heavy with chip and technology companies, has fallen about 40% in less than four months, wiping out roughly US$480 billion in value, according to Reuters figures cited by Techweez. Investors doubt that the money poured into AI chips and data centres will earn enough to justify it: some chipmakers lost much of their value soon after strong listings, and a report found many Chinese data centres underused. Falling share prices do not mean the technology is failing, but they show markets now want profits, not just spending. Alphabet's results this week will test the same question in the US.

Anthropic starts developing its own medicines, beginning with a rare childhood muscle disease

A day after filing to list its shares, Anthropic said it has started its own drug-discovery programmes, led by an experimental antibody for a rare form of muscular dystrophy that affects children from birth. In mice, one version roughly doubled muscle strength, but the study was small, not blinded and not yet peer reviewed, and nothing has been tested in people; Anthropic says human trials could start in late 2028 at the earliest. It bought Mithril Medicine, a one-person company whose founder used Claude to run experiments across 11 outside labs. Drug companies including Bristol Myers Squibb already use Claude to review trial plans and documents.

GADGETS

OPPO's Reno16 arrives in Kenya at KES 79,999, and the Reno16 F at KES 69,999

OPPO launched both 5G phones in Kenya today. The Reno16 is the smaller of the two, with a 6.32-inch screen and an aluminium frame; the Reno16 F has a 6.57-inch screen. Pre-orders until 4 November come with earbuds, a 10,000mAh power bank, six months of screen protection and three months of Google's Gemini Pro plan with 5TB of storage. If you mainly want value, Tech-ish's ranking of phones from KES 20,000 to 60,000 puts the Galaxy A17 4G, at about KES 20,800 with six years of Android updates, at the top.

RETAIL

Samsung opens a shop in Kitui as phone brands look beyond Nairobi

Samsung has opened a shop-in-shop at City Gate Mall in Kitui town, run with two local retailers, where buyers can try its phones with staff to help. "Kenya's markets are diverse, and our retail strategy must reflect the needs of the consumers we serve," said Brenda Nakhulo Khaimia of Samsung's mobile division.
