Kenya logs 2.7 billion cyber threat events in three months, and the CA confirms a government breach it won't name

The Communications Authority's national cyber team, KE-CIRT/CC, detected 2,696,335,021 threat events between July and September, about 29 million a day and 14.45% more than in the previous quarter, according to its latest quarterly cybersecurity report. Most are automated network scans and probes, but the categories that point at real break-in attempts grew fastest: brute-force password attacks rose 85% to 44.9 million, attacks on web applications rose 58% to 27.4 million and malware rose 20% to 70.8 million, while denial-of-service attacks fell 97%. Government systems are among the main targets in four of the six categories. The report also records, in a summary of the national cybersecurity committee's 23 September meeting, that the e-government sector suffered a breach "earlier this year" and is tightening data protection while working through audit requirements from the Data Protection Commissioner. It does not say which agency was hit, how attackers got in, whether personal data was taken or how many people were affected. In July the ICT Ministry disclosed an incident on the President's website and said it found no evidence of data theft, but the CA report does not link the two. On brute-force attacks, the report says successful compromises through vulnerable databases, weak logins and exposed remote-desktop access gave attackers access to critical systems, without giving figures. It blames poor patching, low awareness of phishing and attackers' growing use of AI, and recommends the basics: prompt updates, multi-factor authentication and well-run firewalls. Detected events are not breaches, but last week's PHP flaws and an 85% jump in password guessing are a reason to check that admin panels and remote access are not open to the internet with a password alone.

DStv cuts its top packages by up to KES 4,400 a month from 5 November

DStv Kenya replaces its six packages with four on 5 November. Starter stays at KES 750; Select costs KES 1,500, KES 50 more than Access but KES 750 less than Family; Sports, which carries the Premier League, Champions League and a new SuperSport Swahili channel, costs KES 3,000, down from KES 4,200 for Compact and KES 7,300 for Compact Plus; and Ultimate, with Formula 1, rugby, tennis and golf, costs KES 7,300, down from KES 11,700 for Premium. The biggest savings go to football fans who were paying for Compact Plus. DStv has not said how existing subscribers will be moved across.

Safaricom launches Family Share and an overdraft for Pochi la Biashara merchants

Family Share adds a monthly pot of minutes or data to a Home Fibre plan, which you split across up to five Safaricom numbers: KES 3,749 buys 40 Mbps fibre plus 750 minutes or 14 GB, and KES 5,100 buys 60 Mbps plus 1,100 minutes or 21 GB. Separately, Fuliza ya Pochi gives Pochi la Biashara merchants who have used the wallet for more than six months an overdraft to pay suppliers or buy stock before customers pay, repaid automatically from incoming payments, on *334# or in My OneApp. Safaricom says about a million micro-businesses qualify, but it has not published the limits or the fees, so check them before borrowing.

Airtel Money's revenue grows 34% to US$830 million in its first results since listing

In its first results since listing in London on 14 October, Airtel Money reported revenue of US$830 million for the six months to September, up 34%, or 25% at constant exchange rates, and profit after tax of US$228 million. Customers rose 19% to 56.7 million and the value of transactions rose 46% to US$128 billion. East Africa, which includes Kenya, brought in US$615 million with 44.1 million customers; there is no separate Kenya figure. Lending and savings grew fastest but are still under 5% of revenue. Chief executive Ian Ferrao says about 75 million Airtel phone customers do not use Airtel Money yet.

INTERPOL's Serengeti III arrests 1,462 people over online fraud across Africa

Police in 21 countries, Kenya among them, arrested 1,462 suspects between 29 June and 31 August in INTERPOL's third Serengeti operation against online scams, ransomware and business email fraud. They took down 10,909 malicious networks and websites, identified 65,929 victims and recovered more than US$105 million, about a quarter of the US$385 million in losses linked to the groups. Uganda broke up call-centre fraud rings with 365 foreign nationals arrested, and Angola seized more than 2,000 illegal crypto mining machines. INTERPOL gave no Kenyan arrest figures, and arrests are not convictions.

Uber Kenya must stay in a case brought by 37 riders whose accounts were switched off

The High Court has refused Uber Kenya's request to be dropped from a case brought by 37 motorcycle riders who say their accounts were deactivated without notice, reasons or evidence, after which the financier of their electric bikes, Greenwheels, repossessed them. Uber Kenya argued that the app is run by Uber B.V. in the Netherlands and that it only promotes the service locally. Justice David Mburu said its role needs to be examined at a full hearing. The ruling decides nothing about whether the riders' rights were breached; the case returns on 1 December.
