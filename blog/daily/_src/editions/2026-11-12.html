Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia chief executive after 33 years; finance chief Colette Kress steps in

Nvidia said on Thursday that Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and a board member, "effective immediately". The company gave no reason. Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim CEO and keeps the finance job while the board looks at internal and external candidates, and Harvey Jones becomes Nvidia's first board chair. Jones said the board "has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team" and expects the product roadmap to continue without interruption. The exit follows a turbulent week. On Sunday, Al Jazeera reports, Huang admitted being unfaithful to his wife and apologised to his family and to Nvidia's staff, and the board opened an independent investigation into reports that he had been targeted by Chinese intelligence. The board says it has no reason to believe national security was compromised, and Al Jazeera notes there is no established evidence that company secrets were. US lawmakers are examining the matter. Nvidia's shares fell more than 14% at one point before recovering some ground. Why it matters here: Nvidia supplies an estimated 90% of the advanced chips that AI models are trained and run on, including the ones behind the tools Kenyans now pay for, so its leadership and its export arrangements affect what AI costs everywhere. Under Kress as CFO, annual revenue went from US$4.13 billion to US$215.9 billion. The next test is on Tuesday 17 November, when Nvidia reports quarterly results.

WORLD

Microsoft's Outlook goes down worldwide, with Teams and Copilot disrupted

Users began reporting that they could not open their mailboxes at about 7pm Kenyan time on Thursday. Microsoft first said the problem was in the Americas, then that any region could be affected, and blamed "elevated load" on some of its infrastructure without giving a root cause. It says there is no sign of a cyberattack. If your work email runs on Microsoft 365, expect delays; the desktop app may still show mail it had already downloaded.

Quickmart opens at KSh10 on its first day on the NSE, 33% above its IPO price

The opening trade valued the supermarket chain at KSh40 billion, against KSh30 billion at the KSh7.50 offer price. By midday the average price was KSh9.26 on 6.32 million shares. The KSh15 billion sale was of existing shares held by the founders and Adenia Partners, so Quickmart itself raised no money. Foreign investors now own 52.9%.

CRYPTO

The IMF asks Kenya to rethink the rule that stablecoin issuers keep reserves in the country

The Fund says the requirement could put off issuers such as Tether and Circle, and suggests letting them hold reserves abroad under strict conditions while limiting how local financial institutions use the tokens. It also wants capital rules, now Sh300 million for stablecoin issuers and Sh100 million for exchanges, scaled to the size of the business. Kenyans moved Sh740 billion in crypto in the year to June, up 38%, says Chainalysis.

Kenya's Satlyt is one of ten startups in Google's first Africa Applied AI Lab

Satlyt, which builds computing infrastructure that processes data on satellites in orbit, was picked from thousands of applicants in more than 45 countries, alongside Nigeria's Intron, Smile ID and Termii, Uganda's Crane AI Labs and Senegal's Tolbi. The cohort gets early access to Gemini and Gemma models, mentoring from Google engineers and guidance from four venture firms, ending with a demo day in Accra in December.

Africa is now the world's most attacked region online, and Kenya ranks fourth among countries

Check Point Research counted an average of 3,834 attacks a week on each African organisation in October, up 38% in a year. Kenyan organisations faced 4,302 a week, up 46%; Nigeria was the most attacked country at 6,676. The report also found about one in 35 workplace AI prompts risked leaking sensitive data, most often financial information.
