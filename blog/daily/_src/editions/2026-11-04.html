Two proposals would make TikTok, Meta and X answer to the CA, with fines of up to 10% of global turnover

Kenya has two proposals in front of it that would bring social media companies under the Communications Authority. The first, the Online Safety Bill, 2026, puts a duty of care on social networks, search engines and other online services used in Kenya, from TikTok, Facebook and Instagram to X and YouTube. They would have to assess at least once a year how their algorithms, size and business model help illegal or harmful content spread, act quickly once they know about illegal content, and treat child safety separately, including grooming, cyberbullying and harmful recommendations. Services likely to be used by children would need parental consent, which would push platforms towards age checks such as card verification or identity cross-referencing, and so towards collecting more personal data. Failing the duty of care could cost up to KES 500 million or 10% of annual global turnover, whichever is higher, and executives who consent to a breach or are grossly negligent face up to KES 10 million in fines, five years in prison, or both. The second, the Kenya Information and Communications (Amendment) Bill, 2026, from the ICT ministry, would add a Section 85C requiring platform providers to register with the CA, publish their terms, run complaint channels and report unlawful activity such as sexual exploitation, cyberbullying and impersonation, with what they did about it. It follows Parliament's February decision to reject a TikTok ban in favour of regulation. Both still have to pass through public participation and Parliament. The Online Safety Bill says platforms need not scan private messages, but critics warn that the threat of fines could push platforms to remove borderline posts, including political criticism and satire, and users get no clear independent route to appeal a takedown.

GAMING

Rise of Fearless, an African battle royale, raises US$1 million

The mobile game, inspired by Ethiopia's victory at Adwa in 1896, has raised about KES 130 million from Epic Edges Capital on a SAFE, with the valuation undisclosed. It says it has passed 15,000 downloads, mostly in Nigeria, Ethiopia and South Africa, and founder Kanessa Muluneh wants 100,000 active players within a year. The money goes to development, hiring, servers and marketing. On iPhone it needs iOS 26 and an 850MB download, so check your phone and data first.

AI

Canva restructures 22% of its staff as AI costs slow its growth

Canva's CEO Melanie Perkins told staff that 1,271 people are affected, some moving roles or to central hubs and the rest leaving, in what she called the hardest moment in the company's 13 years. Canva cut its 2026 growth forecast from 30% to 20% in August, saying the cost of running AI features for mostly free users was too high. Revenue still grew 25% to US$921.9 million in the June quarter, and Canva says it has since cut the cost of each AI task by about 90%.

HEALTH

Helium Health wins US$2.5 million from Google.org for AI maternal care

The Nigerian healthtech was the only Nigerian pick among 15 winners of Google.org's US$30 million AI for Government Innovation challenge. HeliumMum will give community health workers AI risk detection and decision support to spot pregnancy complications early, starting in Lagos and moving to Delta, Kano and Oyo, with a target of 1.9 million women over three years. Nigeria accounts for nearly 10% of the world's maternal deaths.

STARTUPS

African startups use the US for funding, the UAE for operations and the UK for licences

TLP Advisory studied 100 tech companies in Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Egypt, Ghana and Morocco with ties abroad. Of those with a UK link, 84% are incorporated there, 47% operate there and 19% hold UK regulatory licences, against 3% for US-linked and 4% for UAE-linked companies. The US is mostly about incorporating to raise money, while 87% of UAE-linked companies run operations there. The sample was chosen for its overseas ties, so it does not show how common this is across all African startups.

CLOUD

Liquid C2 brings Google Cloud to Kenyan IT resellers through Cloudmania

Liquid C2, Cassava Technologies' cloud and cybersecurity arm, will let Kenyan resellers, managed service providers and system integrators sell and support Google Cloud through its Cloudmania distribution business, with training included. For a Kenyan business, it means buying Google Cloud with support from the IT company it already uses. How revenue is split was not disclosed.
