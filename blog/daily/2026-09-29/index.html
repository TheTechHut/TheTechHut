Kenya Airways puts an AI engine behind its fares

The Daily Savannah · Tuesday, 29 September 2026 · 4 stories

Kenya Airways hands fare-setting to an AI engine, a Moringa survey finds three-quarters of Kenyan employers still at the beginning of building tech capability, and Kaspersky turns up fake iPhone 18 stores taking bitcoin.

Featured · AI

Kenya Airways puts an AI engine behind its fares

The airline has adopted FlyNava's Jupiter 5.0 to support revenue management, pulling demand, seat availability, seasonality, competitor activity and booking patterns into one place so pricing teams can test scenarios and move faster. The airline's stated aims are more competitive fares, quicker fare updates and greater consistency — and its framing of the division of labour is careful: the system surfaces patterns, but teams still interpret market conditions, weigh the wider business and decide how the output gets applied. The problem it is pointed at is genuinely messy. Kenya took 2.65 million visitors in 2025 across holidaymakers, business travellers and diaspora, three groups that book on different horizons at different prices. No performance figures have been published yet, which is the part worth coming back to.

Moringa's survey: 73% of Kenyan employers are still at square one on tech capability

Across 166 senior executives in eight sectors, 73% place their organisation at the Early or Developing stage of technology workforce maturity — while 72% call digital transformation a strategic priority. The gap between those two numbers is the report. 77% use online platforms to upskill staff and only 16% think their approach is very effective; what employers say they need is hands-on learning, training aligned to the business, and schedules that bend. AI engineering is named the hardest capability to build internally by 48% of executives, rising to 62% at large enterprises. A companion study of 350 alumni found 86.3% employed within a year — which suggests the training side can work when the practical component is real.

The iPhone 18 is not on sale yet, but the fake stores are open

Kaspersky has found fraudulent sites impersonating legitimate retailers in English, Arabic and Portuguese, timed to launch excitement. The tells are consistent: 25% discounts, pre-orders for colours and storage tiers Apple never announced, promises of part-payment with the balance deferred, bitcoin accepted so the money cannot be clawed back, and forms harvesting names, phone numbers, emails, WhatsApp handles and banking details. "Offers that seem too good to be true... should be treated as clear warning signs," says Kaspersky's Olga Altukhova. The practical advice is dull and effective — official or established retailers only, check the address bar, use a virtual card, turn on multi-factor authentication, read your statements.

TELCOS

Vodacom puts sign-language interpretation inside the M-PESA app

In Kenya the company has worked with Signs Media Kenya to embed the assistALL mini-app into M-PESA, giving Deaf users on-demand sign-language interpretation while using digital financial services — the detail that matters, because financial services are where exclusion costs money. The wider programme spans a South African Sign Language contact centre and emergency app in South Africa, SASL fonts across Microsoft 365, a twelve-module SASL course built with Deaf educators, sign-language support in Tanzania and Egypt, and lobbying with the African Union for member states to strengthen sign-language recognition in law. "Accessibility must be part of that conversation from the beginning," says chief external affairs officer Ayman Essam. An accessibility conference follows in Cairo on 9 November.
