Safaricom says it detected 1.25 billion intrusion attempts last year and cut fraud incidents by 82%

The Daily Savannah · Thursday, 29 October 2026 · 5 stories

Safaricom says it fought off 1.25 billion intrusion attempts and cut fraud incidents by 82% last year, LOOP launches a business bundle whose quick loans cost 6% to 8% a time, Madica gets US$6 million to back 25 to 30 more African startups, Jumia says two-thirds of its Kenyan shoppers live outside Nairobi, and Alphabet loses a record US$460 billion in a day.

Featured · Security

Safaricom says it detected 1.25 billion intrusion attempts last year and cut fraud incidents by 82%

Safaricom's sustainability report for the year to March, covered this week, gives the most detailed picture yet of what it takes to protect M-PESA. The company says it detected about 1.25 billion attempts to break into its systems, investigated more than 130,000 security events and suffered no confirmed material breach, and that denial-of-service attacks were stopped without hurting customers. On fraud, the number of incidents fell 82%, short of its 90% target, and 46% of reported losses were recovered, including KES 953 million in agent commissions. The biggest single source of fraud is still old-fashioned: people talked into handing over details face to face. SIM-swap fraud through franchise shops fell from about 20 cases a month to fewer than five, and 118 staff were dismissed for fraud. On privacy, Safaricom says it finished masking customer details in person-to-person M-PESA messages for more than 40 million subscribers in March, but its own surveys put trust in how it handles data at 70% for M-PESA and 61% for Safaricom, both up on last year. The scale behind those numbers: 46.4 billion M-PESA transactions worth KES 41.7 trillion, 41.7 million monthly active customers and 18.1 million people using Fuliza, up from 7 million. All the figures are Safaricom's own and the report gives no breakdown of the attacks; it also comes as the CA reports a national rise in attempted break-ins and while a case filed in April challenges Safaricom's use of automated decisions in M-PESA. For customers, the fraud figures say where the risk is: callers and visitors who talk you into sharing a PIN or approving a SIM swap.

FINTECH

LOOP's new business bundle: a till, a card machine and loans, and what they cost

LOOP BIZ+ gives small businesses a QR till that takes LOOP, M-PESA and Airtel Money with no hardware, a card machine for Visa, Mastercard and mobile money that is set up in 24 to 48 hours and pays out to any Kenyan bank, a stock tool, a sales dashboard and a free listing in the LOOP app. After 90 days of sales, merchants can borrow: an overdraft or quick loan of KES 1,000 to 500,000, or a term loan of up to KES 3 million over three years. The costs to watch: quick loans carry a 6% to 8% fee whatever the term, and the same again to roll over, so borrowing every month costs 72% or more a year; the overdraft charges 2% of what you draw plus interest from 15.9% a year. The price of the card machine and the card fees were not published.

STARTUPS

Madica gets US$6 million from Flourish to back 25 to 30 more African startups

Madica, the pre-seed programme that pairs a cheque with hands-on help for founders who are often overlooked by venture investors, has received another US$6 million from Flourish Ventures, extending it by three years. Since 2022 it has backed 18 companies in 10 African countries, from AI and climate to semiconductors and HR; all are majority African-owned, more than half have a female co-founder, and on average they have gone on to raise twice Madica's first investment. It did not say how much it puts into each company or name its Kenyan investments.

COMMERCE

Jumia's Black Friday starts tomorrow, aimed at the two-thirds of shoppers outside Nairobi

Jumia Kenya's Black Friday campaign opens on 30 October, and the company says 67.6% of its active Kenyan customers now live outside Nairobi, led by Kiambu, Mombasa, Nakuru and Kajiado. It plans events at pick-up stations to reach them, with Xiaomi, Hisense, oraimo, Safaricom and others taking part. Discounts were not announced. With Glovo making the same point last week, the growth in Kenyan online shopping is now coming from outside the capital.

Alphabet loses a record US$460 billion in a day as AI spending outruns profit

Google's parent fell 12% after revenue rose 25% to US$127.6 billion but net profit dropped 25% to US$26.2 billion and cash flow turned negative by US$9.1 billion. It spent US$51.3 billion on data centres and equipment in the quarter and says the year could reach US$205 billion. Search advertising grew 12%, but Google says AI answers earn less than ordinary search ads. Cloud sales doubled to US$30.7 billion. Coming after China's tech sell-off, it is the clearest sign yet that investors want AI spending to pay back.
