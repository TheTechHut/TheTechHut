A gang used Claude, Grok and Kimi agents to attack more than 400 banks and payment firms, researchers say

A cybercrime group ran a team of AI agents against more than 400 financial targets across Latin America in a matter of months, according to a report published yesterday by Straiker, a US AI-security startup, and first covered by The Information. Straiker calls the group CyberCartel and says it worked as "Tr4nsHack Team" behind a front company, Hexagon Security Labs Mexico; its targets included Mexican banks, fintechs and payment providers. The system, which the gang called CARLIY0S, was built on PentAGI, an open-source tool for automated penetration testing. Straiker says a Claude agent running in the Cursor code editor acted as commander, Claude Opus models wrote exploit code, scouted targets and argued over which attack to try before an "arbiter" model picked one, and five Grok agents dug passwords and keys out of systems they had broken into. When a model refused, the work passed to Moonshot's Kimi models, and when those refused too, to attack code the gang had written in advance. The trick for getting past the models' safeguards was ordinary: the prompts described every job as an authorised security test, an academic exercise or a bug-bounty report. Straiker got a view inside after the group leaked parts of its own operation; a 24 September snapshot of its stolen-data store held 4,493 records, including 2,911 email addresses, 903 Mexican bank account numbers, 391 API keys, 20 signing keys for login tokens, six backdoors and notes on more than 200 vulnerabilities, some previously unknown. The Information reports that data was confirmed stolen from at least one institution, which is being notified; how much money, if any, was taken is not known. Anthropic says it investigated and cut off the gang's access to Claude, and xAI is investigating the Grok accounts. Straiker sells AI-security products and the findings have not been independently checked, but the method matters beyond Mexico: a few people can now run the reconnaissance and exploit-writing that used to need a team, which is why Straiker's chief executive, Ankur Shah, told The Information that "offense is way ahead of defense right now". For Kenyan banks, saccos and fintechs, the practical lessons are the old ones done faster: rotate API keys and signing keys often and give them short lives, watch for logins from unusual places even when they look local, since this gang routed traffic through residential connections, and treat an AI request framed as a "pentest" as a warning sign, not a permission slip.

Sources: Straiker · The Information · PYMNTS · DigitalToday

GOVERNMENT

The ICT Ministry tells government agencies to patch PHP or take sites offline

A day after PHP's emergency release, the Ministry of Information, Communications and the Digital Economy has told agencies to find every website, app and server running PHP, including ones run by contractors, and update the internet-facing ones first. Anything that cannot be fixed straight away should be cut off from outside access or taken offline, and logs should be checked for odd requests, unexpected files and unfamiliar processes. Firewalls are to be used only alongside updates, not instead of them, and where patching is not possible, ICT directors must record the risk and a date for fixing it. The ICT Authority is coordinating. The advisory sets no deadline and names no affected Kenyan sites; Cloudflare has seen attack attempts, but there is no report of a Kenyan system being breached.

MARKETS

The UK government puts KES 1.94 billion into Quickmart's listing

Britain's Foreign Office has subscribed KES 1.94 billion to the Quickmart share sale through MOBILIST, its programme for backing listings in developing markets, about 13% of the KES 15 billion offer. The IFC has also made a conditional commitment of up to US$15 million, about the same amount, so the two could take nearly 26% of the shares on sale. As we reported on 5 October, the offer is two billion existing shares at KES 7.50, valuing the supermarket chain at KES 30 billion, and the money goes to the selling shareholder, not to Quickmart. The offer closes at 5 PM on 30 October, with trading on the NSE expected from 12 November.

FINTECH

M-PESA Jumbe is live, so shop staff can confirm payments without the owner's phone

Safaricom's Jumbe, which we explained on 6 October, is now switched on. A customer paying a till or Pochi la Biashara gets a proof-of-payment message showing the reference, amount, business and time, with an alias such as NK_74 in place of their full name, number and balance. Merchants can dial *234# to add up to 50 staff, who receive payment alerts but cannot see the balance, PIN or statement, so a waiter or shop assistant no longer has to ask to see the customer's SMS. It works on basic phones. The alias is not anonymous, since initials and two digits can still identify a regular customer, and no fees have been announced.

Safaricom offers small businesses 40 Mbps fibre for KES 3,499 a month

Safaricom's new small-business fibre package runs at 40 Mbps, up from 15 Mbps, for KES 3,499 a month, prepaid or postpaid, with security features included and round-the-clock support on 2222. It can be bought in the MySafaricom app or on *400#. Safaricom did not say what the security features are. Fixed internet subscriptions in Kenya reached 2.84 million in June, up 32% in a year, according to Communications Authority figures.
