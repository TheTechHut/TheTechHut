Almost one in five Kenyans online say they pay for AI, the highest share in the world

The Daily Savannah · Friday, 6 November 2026 · 7 stories

Kenyans online are the likeliest in the world to say they pay for AI, ahead of what they pay for music, Safaricom confirms Boniface Mungania as the head of M-PESA, banks lost a record Sh1.9 billion to fraud last year as card fraud doubled, a court lets Equity freeze Sh303 million and turn crypto into shillings in a payroll fraud case, a new Bill would ban online alcohol sales and ads, the NIFC plans a US$500 million startup fund, and Nigeria tells Tether and Circle to set up locally.

Featured · AI

Almost one in five Kenyans online say they pay for AI, the highest share in the world

In the Digital 2027 report from DataReportal, We Are Social and Meltwater, 18.5% of Kenyan internet users aged 16 and over say they pay for an AI subscription every month, against 9.5% worldwide. That puts Kenya first of 53 economies, ahead of Israel at 14.1%, the UAE at 13.3% and Nigeria at 10.0%; the US is at 6.8% and the UK at 4.9%. The figures come from GWI, which surveyed more than 235,000 people in the second quarter of 2026. Kenya is also unusual in what else it pays for: 12.5% pay for music streaming, below the 22.7% worldwide, so more Kenyans say they pay for AI than for music, the reverse of the global picture. Kenyans lead on digital news too, at 21.3%. In the past month, 58.6% of Kenyans online used ChatGPT and 36.8% used Gemini, and both have cheaper plans here now: ChatGPT Go at US$5 a month and Google AI Plus at KES 900. Treat the headline with some care. Tech-ish's arithmetic turns 18.5% of Kenya's roughly 23.9 million internet users into about 4.4 million payers, more than Microsoft's own estimate of about 3.2 million Kenyans aged 15 to 64 using generative AI at all. An online survey may reach better-off Kenyans, and free offers, such as Google's 12 months of AI Pro for students and three months of AI Plus with new TECNO and Infinix phones, may be counted as subscriptions. What it does show is that among Kenyans online, paying for AI is already normal. Neither OpenAI nor Google publishes Kenyan subscriber numbers.

TELCOS

Safaricom confirms Boniface Mungania as head of M-PESA

Mungania, interim chief financial services officer since August, takes the job permanently from 1 November after regulatory approval, succeeding Esther Masese Waititu, who left in July. He joined Safaricom in 2007 as a customer care representative and has since led M-PESA product development and, most recently, public sector digital transformation. He inherits a business that earned KES 100.1 billion in the last six months, 46.3% of Safaricom Kenya's service revenue, from 27.4 billion transactions.

SECURITY

Kenyan banks lost a record Sh1.9 billion to fraud in 2025, and card fraud doubled

Central Bank figures show losses up 19.4% from Sh1.59 billion in 2024, and from Sh412 million in 2023. Card fraud became the biggest source at Sh645 million, more than double the year before, though from fewer incidents. Mobile banking losses fell to Sh634 million, while online banking and internet scams rose sharply. The CBK also reports banks recovering less of what is stolen. Keep one-time passwords to yourself, and turn on card alerts if your bank offers them.

COURTS

A court lets Equity freeze Sh303 million and turn crypto into shillings in a Sh1.49 billion payroll fraud case

Equity says money was moved out of its salary-processing system between November 2023 and June 2024, routed through other companies' accounts and partly converted to cryptocurrency. The High Court has frozen balances at UBA, Absa, NCBA, Stanbic and Co-op Bank, and ordered crypto held by three defendants on Binance and other platforms to be converted within 10 days, with the CBK holding the proceeds as escrow agent. The claims have not been proven in court.

POLICY

A new Bill would ban online alcohol sales, delivery and online ads, and raise the drinking age to 21

Majority Leader Kimani Ichung'wah's Alcoholic Drinks and Drugs Control Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 had its first reading on 5 November. No licences would be issued for online sales, courier or home delivery, which would hit delivery services and liquor stores on apps such as Glovo, and promoting alcohol online, including on social media, could cost up to KES 500,000 or three years in jail. None of it is law yet.

FUNDING

The Nairobi International Financial Centre plans a US$500 million startup fund

Half of the planned Sh65 billion fund would go to technology and AI startups and the rest to SMEs in other sectors, with local and international venture capital firms investing alongside it. NIFC chief executive Daniel Mainda says it is choosing a co-host and a professional fund manager. No date or first investors have been named, so it is a plan, not money on the table yet.

CRYPTO

Nigeria tells Tether and Circle to set up local companies to sell dollar stablecoins

New Central Bank of Nigeria rules say foreign-currency stablecoins such as USDT and USDC can only be offered to Nigerians through a locally licensed issuer with at least ₦15 billion, about US$11 million, in capital, plus reserves, redemption arrangements and monthly reserve checks. Naira stablecoin issuers need ₦10 billion.
