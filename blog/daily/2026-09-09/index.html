Wednesday, 9 September 2026 Edition Nº 14

A Nairobi battery-swap operator pulls in $33.3 million to go continental, a 500km protected cable is planned along the Kenyan coast, and the system meant to place 1.2 million learners in senior school is buckling on deadline day.

Featured · Funding

ARC Ride raises $33.3m to take Nairobi's battery swapping continental

Novastar Ventures and Norrsken22 led the round, with the IFC, British International Investment, Proparco, Musashi Seimitsu and Talanton joining, plus debt from BII's Kinetic programme and Mirova. The money funds 5,000 more electric motorcycles, deeper coverage in Nairobi and western Kenya, and entry into Ghana, South Africa, Tanzania and Uganda — spent on swap stations, battery lifecycle management, automated swapping and smart charging rather than bikes alone. It is the second battery-swap story out of Nairobi in a fortnight, after SUN Mobility opened its own network to rival manufacturers.

INFRASTRUCTURE

LuLu plans a 500km protected cable from Mombasa to Lamu

INDOI of Mauritius, Kenya's Blue Trade Investments, ARISE Integrated Industrial Platforms, AfriTrade Consulting Group and Kingdom Bank are behind LuLu, a 500km system running Mombasa to Lamu with five landing stations including Vipingo SEZ, Kilifi and Malindi. The pitch is redundancy: a subsea run paired with a parallel protected terrestrial route, 144 fibres and a design capacity of 60Tbps per fibre pair, aimed at governments and mission-critical users. Service is targeted for Q2 2028 subject to contracts, on a 25-year design life.

GOVERNMENT

KEMIS meets its first real deadline — and 1.2 million learners are in the queue

Kenya's replacement for NEMIS became mandatory for the 2026 Grade 10 selection, in which roughly 1.2 million Grade 9 learners pick senior schools and subject combinations before today's cut-off. Schools report failed logins, slow responses, submissions that appear to save but do not, records showing last year's cohort, and schools with capacity missing from the list. The context is money: KES 103.99 billion was previously disbursed on unverified NEMIS data, and the latest capitation had to lean on manual verification. The Ministry has still not said publicly who built KEMIS or on what contract.

GADGETS

Apple's foldable is real, and it is called iPhone Duo

Monday's preview landed close: a $1,999 foldable with a 5.4-inch outer screen and a 7.6-inch inner display, Grade 5 titanium, IP68, the 2nm A20 Pro chip, two batteries quoted at up to 44 hours on the outer screen, and iOS 27 Split View. Pre-orders open 16 October, shipping 23 October. It is also eSIM-only, so anyone buying grey-market in Nairobi cannot simply drop a physical SIM into it. Alongside it came the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, AirPods 5, Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4.

POLICY

The CA wants a digital passport on every box of network kit

Draft Technical Specifications for Network Equipment, 2026 would set the type-approval rulebook for anything connecting to Kenyan telecom, mobile, computer and broadcasting networks: 240V/50Hz platinum-efficiency power, mandatory IPv6, firewalls, intrusion detection, IPSec and TLS, a 5–45°C operating range, no lead, mercury or cadmium, and QR or RFID "Digital Product Passports" carrying each device's environmental footprint. High-risk AI systems would need human safeguards; voice latency is capped under 100ms and data under 400ms. Comments close 30 September.

SKILLS

X kills reply-driven payouts, and Kenyan creators have to reapply

Revenue Sharing — which paid on ad revenue generated in a post's replies, and so quietly rewarded whatever started a fight — closed on 7 September, with final payouts on 11 September. Original Content Rewards opened for applications on 8 September and pays on post performance instead, gated behind an X Premium subscription, 500 verified followers and 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users over a rolling 90 days. Payout and verification details carry over, but the impression threshold is a real bar for Kenyan accounts built on reply volume.
