M-PESA moved KSh41.7 trillion as Safaricom dismissed 118 staff over fraud

The Daily Savannah · Wednesday, 28 October 2026 · 8 stories

Safaricom's 2026 report puts M-PESA at 46.4 billion transactions worth KSh41.68 trillion, while disclosing 118 employee dismissals linked to fraud. Across the continent, Madica adds capital for overlooked founders, Taja crosses 50,000 users, Tanzania’s Medikea raises fresh healthcare funding, and Kenyan electric-mobility company Spiro expands into Ghana. Circle Ventures is also backing African cross-border payments infrastructure. And a viral claim that Tala was hacked? False.

Featured · FINTECH & SECURITY

M-PESA moved KSh41.7 trillion as Safaricom dismissed 118 staff over fraud

Two numbers in Safaricom's 2026 Sustainable Business Report tell different parts of the same story. M-PESA processed 46.4 billion transactions worth KSh41.68 trillion in the financial year ended 31 March 2026 — an average of roughly 127 million transactions every day. In that same financial year, Safaricom dismissed 118 employees over fraud-related offences, up from 113 the year before, and referred 14 cases to law enforcement, twice the previous year's seven. The report says fraud was the most common concern raised through its ethics hotline. It lists theft, falsifying company records, unauthorised access to customer call records and security breaches among grounds for dismissal. Safaricom also recovered KSh953 million linked to inflated M-PESA agent commissions, down from KSh1.7 billion a year earlier; the report does not say those recoveries were directly connected to the staff dismissals. The company says it reduced fraud incidents by 82%, short of its 90% target, and recovered 46% of reported losses. It monitored about 1.25 billion cyber-intrusion attempts and investigated more than 130,000 security events. For customers, the scale matters: the platform recorded 99.999% availability and 41.7 million active users in a 30-day period. But trust is not measured only in uptime or transaction volume. Safaricom says its Single View SIM-swap system, which runs 18 automated checks, has reduced fraudulent SIM swaps by 65%. The numbers show both the value of M-PESA to everyday commerce and the continuing work required to protect the people and businesses using it. The figures cover the year ended March, not the current quarter.

STARTUP FUNDING

Madica secures another US$6 million to back up to 30 African startups

Madica, the pre-seed programme affiliated with Flourish Ventures, has secured an additional US$6 million to extend its pilot by three years and support 25 to 30 more early-stage companies. It has backed 18 startups across 10 African countries and 12 sectors since launching in 2022. Its portfolio companies have raised follow-on funding averaging twice Madica's initial investment, according to the programme. The model pairs early capital with hands-on company-building support, targeting founders and markets often missed by traditional venture networks. The new commitment is not a US$6 million grant divided equally among selected startups: it funds investments and support across the expanded portfolio. Madica says applicants should be Africa-headquartered, have a minimum viable product and a few paying customers, and be led full-time by founders with little or no institutional funding.

CROSS-BORDER PAYMENTS

Circle Ventures backs CrissCross as stablecoin payment rails expand across Africa

Circle Ventures, the investment arm of US stablecoin issuer Circle, has invested an undisclosed amount in CrissCross through its parent company Stitch Group. The South African payments infrastructure provider says it operates in more than 30 African markets and supports over 20 currencies. Its platform connects blockchain-based settlement to local bank and mobile-money networks, offering businesses collections, payouts, treasury management and foreign exchange through one API. The investment is intended to expand local payment connections, liquidity and stablecoin settlement for enterprise clients. It is a strategic investment, not a disclosed funding round: neither the amount nor the valuation has been published. Stablecoins may speed up the settlement leg of a cross-border payment, but local currency conversion, regulation and payout networks still matter at the receiving end.

FACT CHECK · DIGITAL LENDING

The viral claim that Tala was hacked is false, Africa Check finds

A graphic circulating on Kenyan social media claimed that hackers associated with the so-called Mulot Boys broke into Tala's lending app and stole millions of shillings. Africa Check traced the mugshot used in the graphic to an unrelated 2024 cybercrime report and found that the image also carried an outdated Tala logo. Tala had labelled the graphic fake on its official Facebook page on 22 October. The fact-check found no evidence for the alleged breach or stolen funds. It is a useful reminder not to forward dramatic financial-security claims based on a screenshot: check the lender's official channels and independent fact-checkers first. This finding addresses the viral graphic; it should not be read as a general guarantee that any digital lender is immune to security incidents.

FINTECH · CROSS-BORDER PAYMENTS

Taja crosses 50,000 users across seven African markets

Pan-African fintech Taja says it has passed 50,000 users and is processing millions of dollars in transactions each month, two years after launching. The platform is live in Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, South Africa, Benin and Côte d’Ivoire, offering multi-currency wallets, international collections, cross-border payouts, real-time conversion, virtual cards and digital-asset settlement. Its growth is another sign that demand for cross-border financial tools is not limited to large companies: creators, freelancers and smaller businesses also need practical ways to receive and move money internationally. Taja says it plans to keep expanding its African footprint and strengthen its payments infrastructure.

HEALTHTECH · TANZANIA

Tanzania’s Medikea raises US$1.2 million to grow its clinic network

Dar es Salaam-based healthtech company Medikea has raised US$1.2 million in pre-seed funding as it expands a model that combines virtual consultations with physical clinics, pharmacies and diagnostic laboratories. The company has opened new clinics in Arusha and Morogoro, bringing its network to four, and aims to reach 10 facilities by the end of next year. Its services span primary care and specialist areas including paediatrics, women’s health, dermatology, fertility and chronic disease management. The round included Baobab Network, Catalyst Fund and develoPPP Ventures, alongside several later investors. Medikea says the capital will support growth, product development and new specialist services, with a target of serving more than 100,000 patients in the next year.

CLIMATE TECH · ELECTRIC MOBILITY

Kenya’s Spiro takes its electric motorcycles to Ghana

Kenyan electric-mobility company Spiro has launched its first 100 electric motorcycles in Ghana, alongside its battery-swapping network. Riders can exchange a depleted battery for a charged one in about two minutes, avoiding the wait associated with conventional charging. The company says it now operates in seven African countries, with more than 156,000 electric motorcycles on the road and over 2,500 battery-swapping stations. Its Ghana entry is part of a wider expansion plan that includes a target of reaching 20 African markets. For commercial riders and delivery fleets, the test will be whether the economics — purchase or lease costs, battery access and daily operating expenses — make electric bikes a viable alternative to petrol-powered motorcycles.
