Vodacom's earnings jump 40% with Safaricom on its books, and 18% once you compare like with like

The Daily Savannah · Wednesday, 11 November 2026 · 6 stories

Vodacom's first results with Safaricom on its books show earnings up 40%, but 18% on a like-for-like basis; Airtel Money customers can now send to 30 banks, SACCOs and microfinance institutions over PesaLink; the government opens a shared, AI-assisted cybersecurity centre at Konza; Mogo's loan book reaches Sh15.9 billion; Samsung becomes Africa's top smartphone seller, helped by daily and weekly instalments; and Pizza Inn's owner buys out its Kenyan delivery arm.

Featured · Telcos

Vodacom's earnings jump 40% with Safaricom on its books, and 18% once you compare like with like

Vodacom Group's results for the six months to 30 September are the first since Safaricom became its subsidiary on 30 June, when the South African operator raised its stake to 55%. EBITDA rose 40% to R42.7 billion (about US$2.6 billion), group revenue 25.6% to R102.5 billion and service revenue 29.4% to R85.1 billion. Much of that is accounting: the comparison period had no Safaricom in it. Assuming Safaricom had been consolidated throughout, EBITDA grew 18% and service revenue 13.3%. On that basis Vodacom is growing more slowly than its rivals: Airtel Africa reported 23.5% underlying EBITDA growth in constant currency for its September half-year, and MTN Group 22.9% over nine months. Safaricom is the engine inside the numbers. Its own half-year showed service revenue of KSh226.1 billion, up 13.1%, EBITDA of KSh129.2 billion, up 27.5%, net income up 35.9% to KSh58.2 billion and operating free cash flow up 59.1% to KSh74.5 billion. Kenya brought in KSh216 billion of the service revenue and Ethiopia KSh10.6 billion. For Kenyans the practical point is who now sets direction: Safaricom's results, and decisions about M-PESA, are consolidated into a Johannesburg-listed group.

PAYMENTS

Airtel Money customers can now send to 30 banks, SACCOs and microfinance institutions over PesaLink

The integration lets a transfer go from an Airtel Money wallet straight into an account at a participating institution, without Airtel striking a separate deal with each one. PesaLink is run by Integrated Payment Services Limited for the Kenya Bankers Association, which says more than 190 institutions are connected to the network.

SECURITY

The government opens a shared cybersecurity operations centre at Konza

Public bodies and private companies can pay an annual fee, based on the servers, firewalls and routers they want watched, for round-the-clock monitoring, threat intelligence, threat hunting and incident response, with AI used to spot suspicious activity. It is aimed at organisations that can't afford their own security team, and competes with managed services such as Safaricom's. It is also one more employer of security analysts.

CREDIT

Mogo's loan book reaches Sh15.9 billion as it doubles its borrowing from Kenyan banks

The asset financier, which lends against motorcycles, tuk-tuks, vehicles and smartphones, disbursed Sh9.2 billion in the first half of 2026. It now borrows Sh1.6 billion from local banks, with total local funding above Sh9 billion. TechTrends reports its motorcycle and smartphone loans are performing very differently. The wider digital credit market doubled in a year to Sh153.8 billion in June.

DEVICES

Samsung becomes Africa's top smartphone seller, with 46% of its revenue from pay-daily and pay-weekly plans

TechInsights says Samsung led the continent by both revenue and units in the second quarter, its unit sales up 34.8% while the market as a whole fell 16.6%. Samsung credits an instalment programme with daily or weekly payments, built for people without a monthly salary. Its share of units was 26.5% in 2025.

DELIVERY

Pizza Inn's owner buys out its Kenyan delivery company, where delivery is now 27% of sales

Zimbabwe's Simbisa Brands paid KES 85.8 million for the last 25% of Kutuma Kenya, which runs Dial a Delivery, the Yum restaurant and grocery app and the Swyft business courier. Simbisa made 3.23 million deliveries in Kenya in the year to June, up from 2.03 million, and wants Kutuma to carry other merchants' orders too, against Glovo, Uber Eats and Bolt Food.
