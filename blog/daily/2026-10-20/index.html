The World Bank wants to rent GPUs to African governments, and names Cassava and Nvidia as partners

The Daily Savannah · Tuesday, 20 October 2026 · 5 stories

The World Bank wants to rent GPUs to governments in Africa and has named Cassava and Nvidia as partners, Kenyan money market funds now average 9.3% as savers drift to riskier funds, Chrome may be holding 4GB of AI model on your laptop, TECNO's Spark 50C arrives with a smaller battery than the phone it replaces, and Pesapal stretches RACK beyond payments.

The World Bank wants to rent GPUs to African governments, and names Cassava and Nvidia as partners

The World Bank Group launched its first digital strategy in Seoul on Tuesday and, with it, an AI Infrastructure and Readiness Initiative to help governments in emerging markets get affordable computing power. The idea is shared, pay-as-you-go access to GPUs, the chips AI runs on, instead of each country building its own national data centre, which the Bank calls costly and slow. Africa and Latin America come first. Cassava Technologies and Nvidia are named as "knowledge partners", but neither the press release nor TechTrends says what that involves, whether either supplies hardware or money, how much the initiative is worth, which countries or sites are included, when it starts, or what access will cost. Kenya is not named. The closest Kenyan link is Cassava's own plan: it said in November 2025 that it had bought 12,000 Nvidia GPUs, a figure it has not independently shown, and its "AI Factory" began running in South Africa this year with Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt and Morocco next. The wider strategy rests on four priorities, connectivity, cheaper smartphones and data, digital ID and payments and skills, and readiness for AI, with an emphasis on "small AI": task-specific tools such as spotting crop disease or helping a shop manage stock. World Bank President Ajay Banga called that "AI for the people". The numbers behind it are the Bank's: more than 2 billion people were offline in 2025, 23% of people in low-income countries use the internet against 94% in rich ones, and developing economies are expected to create about 420 million jobs while 1.2 billion young people reach working age over the next 10 to 15 years.

Sources: World Bank · TechTrends

MONEY

Money market funds now average 9.3%, and savers are moving to riskier funds

Kenyan money market funds returned an average of 9.3% a year after fees as of 15 October, down from 9.9% a year earlier, TechTrends reports; 20 of the 26 funds it tracked are paying less, and 13 still pay double digits, one fewer than last year. The reason is falling rates on what these funds hold: the one-year Treasury bill is at 9.03%, against 9.39% in mid-October 2025, and bank fixed deposits paid 6.91% in August against 7.5% a year before. Savers are following returns into "special funds", which can hold shares, offshore assets and private debt: their share of the KSh 948.7 billion in collective investment schemes rose from 23.9% to 26.6% in the quarter to June, while money market funds fell from 51.9% to 48.5%. Those funds can be harder to cash out quickly and, where they hold dollars, add exchange-rate risk. The article does not say where its fund return figures come from.

HOW-TO

Chrome may have put a 4GB AI model on your laptop, and deleting the folder will not keep it off

Chrome downloads an on-device AI model, reported at more than 4GB, to computers it decides can run it, so features such as writing help, page summaries, tab organising and scam warnings work locally; 9to5Google identifies it as Gemini Nano, which Chrome has offered since 2024, and the size can change with updates. You are not asked first, and the download can come over a metered connection, which matters on bundles and on laptops short of space. To check, type chrome://on-device-internals into the address bar. To stop it, go to Settings, then System, and turn off on-device AI (in some versions it sits under AI Innovations); Google says the model then no longer downloads or updates. In May 9to5Google could not find that setting on a Mac running Chrome 147, though it was there on Windows. Deleting the folder alone does not work: in Techweez's test it came back within minutes. If it keeps returning, set the optimization guide on-device setting in chrome://flags to Disabled and relaunch. Google says prompts to the on-device model are not sent to Google, though some other Chrome AI features do use its servers. None of this is new, Google explained it in May, but Techweez recommends checking the setting again after major updates.

PHONES

TECNO's Spark 50C has a smaller battery and slower charging than the Spark 40C

TECNO's new budget pair goes in opposite directions. The Spark 50s 5G, launched in India on 16 October with sales from today, has a MediaTek Dimensity 6400, a 6,500mAh battery, 45W charging, a 50MP main camera and a 6.78-inch 120Hz screen. The 4G Spark 50C, on a global spec sheet, keeps the Spark 40C's Helio G81 chip and 13MP camera but drops to a 5,200mAh battery, about 13% less than the 40C's 6,000mAh, and 15W charging, down from 18W, in exchange for a larger screen, Android 16 and an IP68 and IP69 rating that Tech-ish has not tested. Its advertised 12GB of RAM is 4GB of real memory plus 8GB borrowed from storage. Neither phone has a Kenyan price or launch date, and storage and NFC vary by market, so check the local version before buying. Tech-ish's verdict: Spark 40C owners have little reason to upgrade.

FINTECH

Pesapal stretches RACK from payments into stock and sales software for shops and restaurants

Pesapal is pushing RACK, its business software, beyond taking payments: retailers can track sales across several outlets, keep stock levels updated as they sell and reconcile payments automatically, and restaurants and bars get order management and checkout tools for busy periods. Pesapal supplies the mobile money and card payments underneath, and Drift Consult has been brought in to advise businesses on setting it up and to find the gaps in how they run. Founder Agosta Liko said businesses need "visibility across their operations". No price, number of businesses using it, countries or launch date was given, and TechTrends notes the value depends on how well it fits the way a business already works and whether owners use the data.
