Kenya's biggest banks haven't lost money in five years, and most small businesses never ask them for a loan

The Daily Savannah · Sunday, 8 November 2026 · 6 stories

Kenya's biggest banks haven't had a losing year in five, while small businesses rarely ask them for loans and Fuliza grows fastest, Nedbank completes its takeover of NCBA, East African ministers adopt an AI strategy that puts AI basics into every university degree, WayaWaya and Choice Bank launch an app that rearranges itself around how you use money, I&M discounts forex in its app on Tuesday and Friday mornings, and Airtel's Gopal Vittal gets another term as GSMA chair.

Featured · Banking

Kenya's biggest banks haven't lost money in five years, and most small businesses never ask them for a loan

TechCabal went through five years of audited results from Kenya's tier-1 banks looking for a bad year and found none. Their combined pre-tax profit reached KES 260.09 billion in 2024 and KES 305.95 billion in 2025, up 17.6%. NCBA's profit after tax grew 409% from 2020 to KES 23.4 billion last year. Part of the reason is where the money goes: by late 2024 government securities made up 27.8% of banking assets and the loan-to-deposit ratio had fallen to 70.1%, while non-performing loans reached 16.4% of the book in December 2024. Banks did lend KES 326.5 billion to small businesses in 2025, more than 73% of it without collateral, but women-led businesses, 46% of borrowers, got 25% of the credit. A 2026 Kenya Bankers Association paper suggests the bigger gap is at the door: about three-quarters of small firms that could use financing never apply, expecting rejection or high costs, while more than 90% of those that apply are approved. The growth is in small, automatic credit. Fuliza, which Safaricom runs with NCBA and KCB, lent KES 1.16 trillion in the six months to September, up 84%, recovered 99.6% of it through M-PESA deductions, and earned Safaricom KES 4.9 billion, up 86%. Its borrowers rose 88.9% to 17.2 million, and the average advance fell to KES 192. If you run a business, the data says applying is worth it more often than people think.

Sources: TechCabal

BANKING

Nedbank completes its takeover of NCBA, and NCBA's old shareholders now own 8.37% of Nedbank

Eligible NCBA shareholders received 43.6 million new Nedbank shares plus KSh23.2 billion in cash. Nedbank now holds 66% of NCBA, and the remaining 34% keeps trading on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. The South African bank gets a lender in six African markets whose digital lending, largely Fuliza, disbursed KSh1.4 trillion in 2025 and made KSh8.9 billion in profit.

TechTrends

AI

East Africa adopts an AI strategy that would put AI basics into every university degree

Ministers at the EAC's sectoral council in Kampala approved an AI Foundational Module for all undergraduate programmes, whatever the subject, and a Regional AI Network of Universities, whose ready members may pilot it first. The Inter-University Council for East Africa will write the rollout plan, and the bloc will develop benchmarks for certifying AI that works in Kiswahili. Each country still has to decide whether to adopt the module, so nothing changes in lecture halls yet.

FINTECH

WayaWaya and Choice Bank launch a banking app that rearranges itself around how you use money

WayaWaya Banking offers accounts, multi-currency wallets, local and international transfers and bill payments under Choice Bank's CBK licence, while WayaWaya builds the app. It uses AI on your transactions to decide what to show first: cash flow for a trader, budgeting for a salaried worker, and payment tools once a salaried user starts a side business.

MONEY

I&M sells discounted forex in its app every Tuesday and Friday morning this month

The FX Flash Deal runs through November from 10am to 1pm on those days in the I&M OTG app, aimed at holiday travel, school fees abroad and supplier payments. A transaction has to be submitted before 1pm; anything after, even if started earlier, gets the standard rate.

TELCOS

Airtel's Gopal Vittal is re-elected GSMA chair, with MTN's Ralph Mupita staying as co-deputy

Vittal, executive vice chairman of Bharti Airtel, will chair the global mobile operators' association through December 2028. MTN's Ralph Mupita and Vodafone's Margherita Della Valle keep their co-deputy chair seats, so two of the three leaders run large African businesses.
