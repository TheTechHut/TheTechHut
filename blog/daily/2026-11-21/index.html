Kenyan startups raised 73% less this year, the lowest in six years, as investors back fewer, bigger deals

The Daily Savannah · Saturday, 21 November 2026 · 5 stories

Kenyan startups raised 73% less in the first nine months of 2026 than a year earlier, the lowest in six years; savers can earn anywhere from 2.9% to 10.9% on a fixed deposit depending on the bank; Equity's businesses outside Kenya now earn half its profit; Safaricom's CTO says the telco is “just scratching the surface” with AI; and TikTok and the ICC bring a free online-selling course to Kenyan small businesses.

Featured · Startups

Kenyan startups raised 73% less this year, the lowest in six years, as investors back fewer, bigger deals

Kenyan startups raised US$201 million (about KSh26 billion) between January and September, down from US$744 million a year earlier, according to Africa: The Big Deal. Only 45 Kenyan startups raised US$100,000 or more, down 32%. Part of the fall is accounting: electric-motorbike company Spiro's more than US$327 million is now counted as Pan-African rather than Kenyan. But the pattern across Africa is clear: total funding barely moved, at US$2.16 billion, while deals of US$10 million or more took 83% of it, and the number of startups raising US$100,000 to US$1 million fell 22%. Nigeria raised US$635 million, more than double last year. Energy startups, a Kenyan strength, raised 74% less. Business Daily counts at least 13 Kenyan startups that collapsed in five years after raising a combined US$717.5 million, including Twiga Foods, which entered administration in September, Copia and Koko Networks. Local money has not filled the gap: pension funds held KSh2.8 trillion at the end of 2025 but put just over 1% into private equity, against a 10% limit. Why it matters here: fewer early-stage rounds mean fewer new startup jobs in 2027. If you are job hunting, favour startups that are already earning revenue, and ask in interviews how much runway they have.

SAVINGS

A fixed deposit earns 2.9% at one bank and 10.9% at another

Central Bank data for September shows African Banking Corporation paying 10.9%, Development Bank of Kenya 10.01% and Middle Eastern Bank 9.22%, while big banks paid much less: Equity 6.68%, KCB 6.37%, Co-op 5.79%, NCBA 5.41% and Standard Chartered 2.91%. Money market funds averaged 9.3% and the one-year Treasury bill about 9.07%. Before you lock money away at your own bank, compare.

BANKING

Equity's businesses outside Kenya now earn about half its profit

Profit after tax rose 32% to KSh68.7 billion in the nine months to September, and regional subsidiaries contributed 49.2% of group profit before tax. CEO James Mwangi says the Congo business, bought in 2020, now returns 23% on equity. Nearly 90% of Equity's transactions are digital, and the bank says it plans to raise its dividend in line with earnings.

AI

Safaricom's CTO says the telco is “just scratching the surface” with AI

James Maitai says AI is central to Safaricom's goal of becoming a technology company by 2030, starting with Zuri, the assistant in the My Safaricom app. In the six months to September the company built more than 500 new sites, over 200 of them 5G, and connected more than 120,000 homes to fibre. AI, data and network roles at Safaricom are worth watching on our jobs board.

SMALL BUSINESS

Kenyan small businesses can take TikTok and the ICC's free course on selling online

The Digital Commerce Labs, run by TikTok and the International Chamber of Commerce, held their first Kenyan event in Nairobi this week. There is a free online course of four to five hours with a certificate, and live virtual classes for Kenya are planned for early 2027. A 2023 GSMA survey found 49% of Kenyan businesses sold only through social media. Registration is through an ICC form, and event places are limited.
