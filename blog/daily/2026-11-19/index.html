Anthropic opens on Nasdaq 34% above its IPO price after raising more than US$81 billion

The Daily Savannah · Thursday, 19 November 2026 · 6 stories

Anthropic opens on Nasdaq 34% above its IPO price after raising more than US$81 billion; Visa hires former M-PESA boss Esther Waititu to run 24 markets including Kenya; the Nairobi Expressway lost KSh5.1 billion in 2025 even as traffic grew; hackers leak data on thousands of Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan cardholders from outside vendors; Zipline hires Uber's Anthony le Roux to lead an African expansion it says will create 800 jobs; and Google Play will make apps work properly on foldables from April 2027.

Featured · Markets

Anthropic opens on Nasdaq 34% above its IPO price after raising more than US$81 billion

The maker of Claude priced 980 million shares at US$83 on Wednesday, above the US$70 to US$78 range it had planned and with more shares than the 825 million it first offered. That raised about US$81.34 billion, which tech-ish calls a record. When trading began on Thursday under the ticker ANT, the first trade was at US$111.50, at about 7:48pm Kenyan time. The stock touched US$114.99 before easing to about US$96.90 by 9:45pm. A separate US$26 billion private placement to Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, also at US$83, sits outside the IPO count. TechCrunch reports the opening price implied a market value above US$2 trillion. The filing shows revenue of US$33.24 billion in the nine months to September, against US$2.55 billion a year earlier, and about 5,300 employees. Each of the seven co-founders sold about US$280 million of shares and keeps a stake worth roughly US$20 billion at the IPO price. “Today puts the company in a stronger financial position,” chief executive Dario Amodei said before the bell. For Kenyans: no African country was on the retail list, so there was no way to apply at US$83, but anyone whose broker or app offers US shares can now buy at the market price, which may be well above it.

Sources: tech-ish · TechCrunch

PAYMENTS

Visa hires former M-PESA boss Esther Waititu to run 24 markets, Kenya among them

Waititu, who left Safaricom on 31 July after three and a half years as Chief Financial Services Officer, becomes Visa's Senior Vice President for Southern and Eastern Africa, based in Johannesburg. Under her, M-PESA brought in KSh182.7 billion in the year to March and Safaricom completed Fintech 2.0. Visa has given no start date or Kenya targets. Kenya had 13 million payment cards in May against 53 million active mobile money subscriptions, which is the gap Visa is trying to close.

The Nairobi Expressway lost KSh5.1 billion in 2025 even as more cars used it

The Treasury's annual PPP report shows the operator, Moja Expressway, earned KSh9.63 billion against costs of KSh14.73 billion, a loss four times larger than the year before. Traffic kept rising, to about 80,000 vehicles a day in April to June. The worst-case bill to the government under the 30-year contract stands at KSh87.6 billion. Treasury CS John Mbadi said the reasons costs are rising need a closer look.

SECURITY

Hackers leak data on thousands of Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan cardholders, but no card numbers

An extortion group published files taken from outside marketing and loyalty vendors, not the companies' own systems: names, emails and booking details for more than 6,000 premium Visa cardholders in Asia, and about 16,000 JPMorgan customer emails. Nothing so far places Kenyan cardholders in the files. Expect convincing phishing that quotes real details; a bank will never ask for your PIN or full card number.

CAREERS

Zipline hires Uber's Anthony le Roux to lead an African expansion it says will create 800 jobs

Le Roux, 13 years at Uber, becomes the drone company's Head of Operations for Africa in January. Zipline, which already flies in Kenya, Rwanda, Ghana, Nigeria and Côte d'Ivoire, plans dozens of new distribution centres over two years, reaching more than 130 million people and 20,000 health facilities, and expects to add more than 800 high-skilled jobs. Worth watching if you work in logistics, operations or engineering.

DEVELOPERS

Google Play will block updates to apps that do not work properly on foldables from April 2027

Apps must fill the whole screen, keep the user's place when a phone is folded, rotated or resized, and work in split-screen, or they will no longer be able to publish updates and may be shown less often. New listings will also need three foldable screenshots. Games get until August 2027. If you build Android apps for clients, add large-screen testing to next year's plan.
