Cloud9 raises US$500,000 from Alliance to expand cross-border payments

The Daily Savannah · Thursday, 8 October 2026 · 3 stories

A Kenyan stablecoin-rails fintech lands US$500,000 from a New York accelerator, a payments API wins its Mozambique licence, and Spotify's audiobook rollout names Kenya but not a price.

Featured · Funding

Cloud9 raises US$500,000 from Alliance to expand cross-border payments

The New York accelerator's cheque is part of Cloud9's pre-seed round and takes its disclosed funding to US$1 million. Founded in late 2025 by Tesh Mbaabu and Mesongo Sibuti — both co-founders of the collapsed e-commerce startup Marketforce — it launched in early 2026 as a digital bank for people and businesses that transact across currencies and borders. Users can hold shillings, dollars, euros, pounds and yuan; businesses can pay suppliers in more than 100 countries, receive payments into dedicated virtual accounts and run payroll, bulk payments and team approvals; and Cloud9 Wealth offers savings products and global stocks. Cross-border transfers settle in USDC and USDT stablecoins, which is how it avoids needing banking relationships in each country. Cloud9 claims more than 25,000 accounts since going live and weekly transaction growth above 15%. The money goes to new corridors, the platform, virtual and physical cards, and customer acquisition. It also incorporated social-commerce platform Chpter in August and earlier bought ticketing company M-Tickets for about KSh100 million.

FINTECH

PawaPay gets a Bank of Mozambique licence to onboard merchants directly

The licence went to its local subsidiary, Quidexplus Mozambique, and lets PawaPay sign up Mozambican businesses and process their payments under central bank supervision; onboarding should start "in the coming days". The company, founded in 2020, sells one API to mobile money wallets in 23 African countries and now handles about 7 million transactions a day. Mozambique is a mobile-money market — 24.6 million mobile money accounts against 6.7 million bank accounts at the end of November 2025 — but coverage is thin: merchants can collect from Vodacom's M-Pesa and pay out to Movitel's e-Mola, while e-Mola collections and mKesh have no announced timeline. The article could not verify PawaPay's "one of the first" claim, since there is no public register. The Kenyan angle: its Kenya unit has CBK authorisation, but PawaPay's Kenya documentation lists only M-Pesa, and only for payouts.

MEDIA

Spotify says audiobooks reach Kenya by year-end — price and hours still unknown

Announced on 7 October at the Frankfurt Book Fair, the rollout takes audiobooks from 22 markets to more than 180 by the end of 2026, with Kenya on the list alongside Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Ethiopia, Nigeria and Egypt. Access is measured in hours, not titles, and in Africa the expected route is Audiobooks+, a paid add-on; only South Africa gets hours inside Premium. As of 8 October, Spotify's Kenyan audiobooks page redirects to the Premium plans page, which lists no audiobook option, and Kenya's Individual plan stays at KES 419 a month. The Kenyan price of Audiobooks+, its hours, whether free users can add it and whether Swahili titles are coming are all unanswered. For scale, US Audiobooks+ is US$11.99 for 15 hours. Spotify says the add-on has passed 1 million subscribers and US$100 million in annual recurring revenue.
