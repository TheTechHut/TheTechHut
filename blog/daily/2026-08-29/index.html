Meta starts charging Kenyan businesses for every WhatsApp reply

The Daily Savannah · Saturday, 29 August 2026 · 7 stories

Meta puts a price on the business chat window Kenyan banks and retailers have leaned on for free, Nairobi applies to join the bloc writing the world's digital trade rules, and the half-year books show how much of Kenyan banking has already moved onto the phone.

Featured · Fintech

Meta starts charging Kenyan businesses for every WhatsApp reply

From 1 October, businesses on the WhatsApp Business Platform pay $0.0040 — about KES 0.52 — for each service or utility message sent outside the 24-hour customer window, with Kenya grouped into Meta's "Rest of Africa" band alongside Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda. The free WhatsApp Business app is untouched, but banks, airlines and large retailers running chatbots — Equity's EVA among them — now carry a per-reply cost: roughly KES 52,000 a month on 100,000 replies, with no volume discounts. Click-to-WhatsApp advertisers get a 72-hour free window instead of 24. Payment methods must be on file by 30 September to avoid interruption.

POLICY

Kenya applies to join the Digital Cooperation Organisation

Kenya is one of eight countries — with Albania, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Lebanon, Palestine, Syria and Zambia — cleared to join the DCO, the intergovernmental bloc on digital inclusion and cross-border data flows. Membership takes effect once Nairobi completes accession and deposits its instrument; the expanded bloc would cover 24 members and roughly 980 million people.

FINTECH

I&M's half-year profit climbs 22% as 92% of customers go digital

I&M Group posted net profit of KSh 10.2 billion for H1 2026, on operating income up 23% to KSh 33.7 billion and total assets up 27% to KSh 746 billion. The lender says 92% of its customer base is now digitally active, and regional subsidiaries lifted their share of pre-tax profit to 33% from 25%.

FINTECH

Britam profit jumps 52% as it digitises marine cargo cover

Britam reported pre-tax profit of KSh 3.8 billion, up 52%, with the net insurance service result up 36% to KSh 1.8 billion — growth from underwriting rather than investment gains. The insurer launched a digital marine cargo platform as Kenya moved to mandatory electronic marine cargo certificates in July.

TELCOS

Kenya's carriers are not ready for the next wave of connected devices

With a 5G MacBook rumoured for late 2026, neither Safaricom nor Airtel supports cellular wearables or Wi-Fi calling, and only Safaricom carries 5G alongside eSIM. Bundles are shrinking too — Airtel cut its Smarta 1500 allowance from 90GB to 32GB this month — against 51.5 million mobile broadband subscriptions and just 2.46 million fixed lines.

SKILLS

TikTok leans on Kenya's appetite for STEM content

New GWI research commissioned by TikTok puts technology interest among Kenyan users at 56% and science at 31%, with Kenyan users 16 percentage points likelier than non-users to follow science. The platform is pushing a dedicated STEM Feed and the #LearnOnTikTok tag, now past 51 million video creations.

GOVERNMENT

KE plates roll out as NTSA plays down the switch

NTSA says the move from KD to KE registrations is routine alphabetical progression, not a new system — though the plates feed the automated enforcement and vehicle identification databases built since the 2022 new-generation rollout. Kenya registered 207,259 vehicles between January and June, 133,127 of them motorcycles.
