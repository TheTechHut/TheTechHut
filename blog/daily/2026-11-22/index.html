Visa wants your TECNO, Infinix or itel phone to become a card terminal

The Daily Savannah · Sunday, 22 November 2026 · 5 stories

Visa signs up with Transsion to put tap-to-pay on TECNO, Infinix and itel phones, starting in Africa; Royal Media charges KES 350 a month to stream Citizen TV on YouTube; Simba Corporation launches MG with a KES 1 billion electric-car line in Mombasa; Storspay opens cross-border payroll into Kenya and five other African markets; and TechCrunch tells how three investors bet early on Anthropic.

Featured · Payments

Visa wants your TECNO, Infinix or itel phone to become a card terminal

Visa and Transsion Holdings, the maker of TECNO, Infinix and itel, have signed a memorandum of understanding to explore building Visa's payment technology into Transsion's phones, starting in African markets. There are three parts. Visa Tap to Pay would let owners pay contactlessly with the phone. Visa Tap to Phone would let a compatible smartphone accept card payments without a separate terminal, aimed at micro and small businesses. And Visa Intelligent Commerce, its payments for AI agents, has already been integrated technically and tested with live transactions. No launch date, countries, phone models or merchant fees have been announced, and an MoU is not a launch. The size of the prize is clear, though: Transsion's phones sell in more than 100 countries and regions. The competition is M-PESA, where Safaricom reported 4.5 million merchants on Pochi la Biashara and Lipa na M-PESA in the six months to September, handling 4.7 billion merchant transactions. Why it matters here: card acceptance on a cheap phone could finally make cards practical for small Kenyan shops. If you build payments or merchant tools, this is the integration to watch in 2027.

MEDIA

Citizen TV now charges KES 350 a month to stream live on YouTube

Royal Media Services has put live streams of Citizen TV, Inooro and Ramogi behind a YouTube channel membership, paid by bank, debit, credit or virtual card. It is not YouTube Premium, and Techweez found the rollout uneven, with Inooro and Ramogi still free in spot checks. Citizen holds 56% of TV viewership, per a 2025 Media Council survey, but card-only payment shuts out viewers who live on M-PESA. Techweez works out that 1% of daily viewers would bring about KES 85 million a month before YouTube's cut and taxes.

E-MOBILITY

Simba Corporation launches MG in Kenya with a KES 1 billion electric-car line in Mombasa

Simba is now MG's exclusive Kenyan distributor, launching two fully electric models alongside petrol cars, assembled at Associated Vehicle Assemblers in Mombasa by 60 technicians trained in battery and electrical systems. Simba says MG already took more than 40% of Kenya's new electric car and SUV sales this year. Drive Electric bought 25 vehicles and Kenya Power took four, saying a switch could cut fuel and maintenance costs by up to 70%. EV technicians are one of Kenya's newest skilled trades.

REMOTE WORK

Storspay opens cross-border payroll into Kenya and five other African markets

The Canadian platform lets North American businesses pay staff, contractors and suppliers in Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Benin, to bank accounts or mobile money wallets. It has just registered with Canada's financial regulator, FINTRAC, serves verified businesses only and advertises fees from 1%, commonly 2%. If you work remotely for a North American client, ask what they pay in fees and exchange-rate margin: Techweez notes the real cost depends on both.

INVESTING

How three investors bet early on Anthropic before its record IPO

Notable Capital put about 10% of the US part of its fund into Anthropic's Series E at a US$61.5 billion valuation, the most it had ever paid, after finding developers used Claude 70% of the time. Singapore's GIC invested more than US$4.5 billion across later rounds and holds about 1% after the listing; Baillie Gifford put in US$1.5 billion. TechCrunch puts the paper return at roughly 15 times. The lesson for founders: the investors who won watched what developers actually used.
