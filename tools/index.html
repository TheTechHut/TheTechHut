Part one

Getting money from abroad into Kenya

If you are contracting rather than employed, this is your problem to solve. The question to ask of any service is not "what is the fee" but "can I actually open an account from Kenya, and can I get the money to M-Pesa or a Kenyan bank without losing a week." Check current availability yourself before you commit — these things change country by country, often.

For contractors and freelancers

Wise

Multi-currency account with real exchange rates. Widely used to receive USD, GBP and EUR and convert to shillings without a bank eating the difference. Good for invoicing a foreign client directly.

Check which receiving currencies are open to Kenyan residents before you promise a client an account number — the list is not the same everywhere.

Open Wise
For marketplace and agency work

Payoneer

The default for people getting paid through Upwork, Fiverr and similar platforms, and for agencies paying contractors in bulk. Long-established in Kenya and accepted by most Western payers without argument.

Fees are higher than Wise on straight currency conversion. Use it where the payer insists on it, not as your everyday account.

Open Payoneer
For an African-built option

Grey

Virtual USD, GBP and EUR accounts aimed squarely at African freelancers, with local payout into Kenyan accounts. Built by people who had this exact problem, which shows in the onboarding.

Younger and smaller than the two above. Some foreign payroll teams will not recognise it, so have a fallback.

Part two

When the company wants to employ you properly

An employer of record is a company that legally employs you in Kenya on behalf of your actual employer abroad — so you get a contract, payslips, NSSF and NHIF deductions and leave, instead of invoicing as a freelancer. You do not sign up for these yourself. Your employer does. Knowing the names is the point: when a foreign company says "we would love to hire you but we have no entity in Kenya", these are the three words that unblock it.

Say this name in the interview

Deel

The one most startups already use. If a company is hesitating about hiring in Kenya, "you could use Deel" is often all it takes — the legwork is theirs and it takes days, not months.

Costs the employer a monthly fee per person, so expect it to come up in salary negotiation. Know the number before you negotiate.

Say this name in the interview

Remote

The other name worth knowing, and the one more established companies tend to pick. Same idea: they employ you locally and bill your employer.

Same per-person cost to the employer. Smaller companies sometimes balk, which is when a contractor arrangement is the realistic fallback.

Hiring into Africa

Oyster

Markets itself specifically at companies hiring across borders into places they have no entity, and it is currently hiring EMEA roles itself — it appears on our remote list.

Less widely recognised in Kenya than the first two. Lead with Deel or Remote and keep this one in reserve.

What Oyster is
✅ The sentence that gets people hired. When a foreign employer says they cannot hire in Kenya, reply: "You do not need an entity here — Deel, Remote and Oyster all employ people in Kenya on your behalf, and it takes about a week." Plenty of hiring managers do not know this. Being the candidate who solves their problem is worth more than another line on your CV.
Part three

Learning that is worth paying for

Most of what you need is free. Pay only where the structure, the feedback or the certificate genuinely buys you something — and be honest with yourself about which of the three you are actually after.

Certificates employers recognise

Coursera

Worth it for the professional certificates that show up by name in job descriptions — data analytics, cloud, project management. Financial aid is real and routinely granted; apply for it before paying full price.

A certificate is a tiebreaker, not a qualification. It will not outweigh a portfolio, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling a course.

Browse Coursera
Data and analytics

DataCamp

Short, browser-based exercises in Python, SQL and R. Good for drilling syntax until it is automatic, which is exactly what a technical screen tests.

Teaches you to complete exercises, not to build things. Pair it with a real project or it will not change your CV.

Browse DataCamp
One specific gap

Udemy

Best used surgically: you need Docker by Friday, you buy the Docker course on sale for a few hundred shillings. Never pay list price — there is always a sale.

Quality swings wildly between instructors. Read the recent reviews, not the rating, and check when the course was last updated.

Browse Udemy
Part four

Hosting the thing you built

A portfolio link beats a portfolio section. These are the cheapest credible ways to put something live on your own domain and keep it there.

A server you control

DigitalOcean

A small droplet runs a side project comfortably and teaches you more about Linux, deployment and DNS than any course will. Student credit is often available.

You are now a sysadmin. Set up billing alerts on day one — a forgotten droplet is a quiet monthly charge.

See DigitalOcean
Cheap shared hosting

Hostinger

If you just need a WordPress site or a static portfolio online for very little money, this is the pragmatic answer. Accepts cards and works fine from Kenya.

The headline price is the multi-year rate. Renewal costs considerably more — diarise it before it surprises you.

See Hostinger
Your name, your domain

Namecheap

Buy yourname.com and put your email on it. Applying from a personal domain instead of a numbered Gmail is a small thing that quietly signals seriousness.

First-year pricing again. And do not buy eleven domains for projects you have not started — we have all done it.
