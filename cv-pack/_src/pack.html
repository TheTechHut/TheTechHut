A robot reads your CV first. Here is which one.

Safaricom runs Oracle. Britam runs Taleo. M-KOPA and Andela run Ashby. Tala runs Lever. Each of those systems reads your CV differently, and formatting that sails through one gets you silently filtered by another. We checked the live job feed of every major Kenyan tech employer we track and wrote down what we found.

Part one · the map

Every row was confirmed against the employer's own live careers feed in October 2026. Employers do change systems, so treat this as a snapshot.

The pattern worth memorising: if the apply link goes to oraclecloud.com or taleo.net, you are in a form-first system: the fields are everything. If it goes to greenhouse.io, lever.co, ashbyhq.com or pinpointhq.com, your CV file is parsed and searched directly, so formatting is everything.
Part two · the rules

Six formatting rules that satisfy every system above

Rule 01

One column. Always.

Two-column CVs, the ones with a coloured sidebar, are read left to right across the page by most parsers. Your job titles end up merged with your skills list.

Rule 02

No tables, text boxes or icons.

Taleo drops table contents entirely. Icons become nothing: if a phone icon replaces the word "Phone", some parsers lose your number. Write the words.

Rule 03

Use the boring headings.

"Work Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Certifications". Parsers match headings against a fixed list; a creative heading means that section is never categorised.

Rule 04

Dates as MM/YYYY, right of the role.

03/2024 – Present. Not "Mar '24 to date". Dates are how systems calculate your years of experience, and that number is often a hard filter.

Rule 05

Mirror the posting's exact nouns.

If the ad says "Node.js", write Node.js, not NodeJS. Keyword matching is literal. Put the stack in a Skills line and inside the bullet where you used it.

Rule 06

Submit a PDF, unless asked for Word.

Modern systems parse PDF cleanly; Taleo and some Oracle set-ups still prefer .docx. Keep both, named Firstname-Lastname-Role.pdf, never "CV final v3".

The 60-second test: open your CV, select all, copy, and paste into a blank plain-text note. That is almost exactly what the parser sees. If the order is scrambled, your dates vanish or your name is missing, that is your rejection, before any human was involved.
