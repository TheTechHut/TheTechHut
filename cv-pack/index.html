A robot reads your CV first. Here is which one.

Safaricom runs Oracle. Britam runs Taleo. M-KOPA and Andela run Ashby. Tala runs Lever. Each of those systems chews through your CV differently — and the formatting that sails through one gets you silently filtered by another. Part one · the map

Who runs what

Every row below was confirmed against the employer's own live careers feed in October 2026. Employers do change systems, so treat this as a snapshot — but the pattern holds: the funded startups sit on modern, parser-friendly systems, and the big corporates sit on Oracle products that make you re-type your whole life into a form.

Zipline Greenhouse Posts Kenya roles alongside global ones on one board. Filter by location before you apply or you will waste a week.

Sun King Pinpoint Short, forgiving form. Volume is high across Africa — being specific about your county and language skills genuinely helps.

⚠️ The pattern worth memorising: if the apply link takes you to a URL containing oraclecloud.com or taleo.net, you are in a form-first system — the CV is secondary and the fields are everything. If it takes you to greenhouse.io, lever.co, ashbyhq.com or pinpointhq.com, your CV file is parsed and searched directly, so formatting is everything.
Part two · the rules

Six formatting rules that satisfy all seven systems

You cannot keep a different CV per employer. You do not need to. These six rules are the intersection — the format that parses cleanly in Oracle, Taleo, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Pinpoint and JazzHR at the same time.

RULE 01

One column. Always.

Two-column CVs — the ones with a coloured sidebar — are read left-to-right across the page by most parsers. Your job titles end up merged with your skills list. This single choice breaks more Kenyan applications than anything else.

RULE 02

No tables, text boxes or icons.

Taleo drops table contents entirely. Icons and logos become nothing. If a phone icon replaces the word "Phone", some parsers lose your number. Write the words.

RULE 03

Use the boring headings.

"Work Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Certifications". Not "Where I've Made Impact". Parsers match section headings against a fixed dictionary; a creative heading means that section is never categorised.

RULE 04

Dates as MM/YYYY, right of the role.

03/2024 – Present. Not "Mar '24 to date", not seasons, not just years. Date parsing is how systems calculate your years of experience, and that number is often a hard filter.

RULE 05

Mirror the posting's exact nouns.

If the ad says "Node.js", write Node.js — not Node, not NodeJS. Keyword matching is literal. Put the stack in a Skills line and inside the bullet where you used it.

RULE 06

Submit a PDF — unless asked for Word.

Modern systems parse PDF cleanly. Taleo and some Oracle configurations still prefer .docx. Keep both exports of the same file, named Firstname-Lastname-Role.pdf, never "CV final v3".

✅ The 60-second test: open your CV, select all, copy, and paste into a blank plain-text note. What you see is almost exactly what the parser sees. If the order is scrambled, your dates vanish or your name is missing — that is your rejection, before any human was involved.
